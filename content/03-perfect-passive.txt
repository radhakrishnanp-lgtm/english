# 15 | Present perfect: I have done | Present perfect | A2
summary: have/has + past participle. Use it for a past action with a result now: I've lost my keys (I don't have them now).
[learn]
## Form: have / has + past participle
| | | |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | **have** ('ve) | finish**ed** / lost / seen |
| he / she / it | **has** ('s) | finish**ed** / lost / seen |

The past participle of regular verbs ends in **-ed** (the same as the past simple): cleaned, opened, finished.
Irregular verbs: go → **gone**, see → **seen**, eat → **eaten**, write → **written**, break → **broken**, lose → **lost**, buy → **bought**, do → **done**.

## Use: a past action with a result **now**
= I**'ve lost** my keys. (= I don't have them now.)
= She**'s broken** her arm. (= Her arm is broken now.)
= They**'ve gone** home. (= They aren't here now.)
= Look! Someone **has opened** the window.

We are thinking about **now**, not about when it happened.

## Negative and questions
= I **haven't finished** my homework.
= He **hasn't called** me.
= **Have** you **seen** my phone? — Yes, I **have**. / No, I **haven't**.
= **Has** the film **started**? — No, it **hasn't**.

## gone or been?
| | |
|---|---|
| He has **gone** to Paris. | He is in Paris now (or on the way). |
| He has **been** to Paris. | He went there and came back (some time in his life). |
[examples]
I've cleaned my room. Doesn't it look nice? :: result now
Oh no! I've forgotten my password.
The price of petrol has gone up again.
Have you finished your coffee? — Yes, I have.
Rahul has bought a new bike.
We haven't decided yet.
My sister has gone to the shops. She'll be back soon. :: gone = not here now
The rain has stopped. Let's go out.
[mistakes]
I have lose my wallet. => I have lost my wallet. :: have + past participle
She have finished. => She has finished. :: she → has
Have you saw my glasses? => Have you seen my glasses? :: see → seen

fill: ___ you ___ (see) my phone? = Have, seen :: Have + you + seen

order: We have bought a new car. :: have + past participle
[medium]

fix: I have broke my glasses. => I have broken my glasses. | I've broken my glasses. :: break → broken
mc: Has the shop opened? — No, it ___. | didn't | *hasn't | haven't :: it → hasn't
fill: Somebody ___ (take) my umbrella! = has taken | 's taken :: take → taken
mc: Mina isn't here. She ___ to the bank. | has been | *has gone | was gone :: gone = still there

# 16 | Present perfect: just, already, yet | Present perfect | A2
summary: Use just (a short time ago), already (sooner than expected) and yet (until now, in questions and negatives) with the present perfect.
[learn]
## just = a short time ago
**just** goes between *have/has* and the participle.
= I**'ve just** had lunch, so I'm not hungry.
= "Where's Tom?" "He**'s just** left."

## already = before now, sooner than expected
= "Don't forget to call Mum." "I**'ve already** called her."
= The film **has already** started. We're late!

## yet = until now
Use **yet** in **negatives** and **questions**. It usually goes at the **end**.
= I **haven't** finished **yet**. (but I will)
= **Have** you **done** your homework **yet**?
= "Has the bus come?" "No, not **yet**."

| | Position | Sentence type |
|---|---|---|
| **just** | before the participle | positive |
| **already** | before the participle | positive (and questions) |
| **yet** | end of sentence | negative and questions |

> In American English people often use the past simple: I **just ate**. Did you eat **yet**? In this course we practise the British style with the present perfect.
[examples]
I've just woken up.
The parcel has just arrived.
We've already seen this film.
They've already sold all the tickets.
I haven't read your message yet.
Has your brother found a job yet?
"Are you ready?" "Not yet!"
She's already spent all her money.
[mistakes]
I have finished yet. => I have already finished. | I've already finished. :: yet is for negatives and questions.
I've finished just. => I've just finished. :: just goes before the participle.
Did you finish yet? => Have you finished yet? :: Present perfect with yet (British English).

fill: I haven't paid the bill ___. = yet :: negative → yet at the end
fill: She's ___ left. You missed her by one minute. = just :: a moment ago
order: We have already eaten. :: already before the participle
[medium]
fix: I have yet seen this film. => I have already seen this film. | I've already seen this film. :: positive → already
fill: ___ you ___ (book) the hotel yet? = Have, booked :: Have + you + participle + yet
mc: Do you want a sandwich? — No thanks, I've just ___. | ate | *eaten | eat :: have just + eaten
fill: The children ___ (not / wake up) yet. = haven't woken up | have not woken up :: wake → woken
order: Has the post arrived yet? :: yet at the end
fix: He has went already. => He has already gone. | He's already gone. :: go → gone
[hard]

# 17 | Have you ever…? (present perfect for experiences) | Present perfect | A2
summary: Use the present perfect to talk about experiences in your life until now, with ever, never, before, once, twice, many times.
[learn]
## Experiences in your life (until now)
We don't say *when* it happened, only *if* it happened.
= I**'ve been** to Singapore.
= She**'s met** a famous actor.
= We**'ve never seen** snow.

## ever and never
**ever** = at any time in your life (in questions)
= **Have** you **ever eaten** sushi?
= **Has** she **ever been** to Kashmir?

**never** = not at any time
= I**'ve never driven** a truck.
= He **has never been** on a plane.

## How many times?
**once**, **twice**, **three times**, **many times**, **several times**
= I've read that book **twice**.
= We've been to Ooty **many times**.

## The first time…
= This is the **first time** I**'ve eaten** this fruit.
= It's the **first time** she**'s been** abroad.

## Present perfect → past simple
Start with the experience (present perfect). When you give details about **when**, change to the **past simple**.
= "**Have** you **ever been** to London?" "Yes, I **have**. I **went** there in 2019."
[examples]
Have you ever ridden a horse? — No, never.
I've never tried Thai food.
My parents have been to Mecca twice.
She's worked in three different countries.
Have you ever lost your passport?
This is the first time I've flown alone.
I've seen this movie five times!
He's never been late for work. :: never + been
[mistakes]
Did you ever eat snails? => Have you ever eaten snails? :: Experience → present perfect.
I've been to Delhi last year. => I went to Delhi last year. :: Finished time → past simple.
I have never went there. => I have never been there. | I've never been there. :: go → been (experience)

[medium]

# 18 | How long have you…? (present perfect for unfinished time) | Present perfect | A2
summary: Use the present perfect (have been / have known / have lived…) with How long, for and since for situations that started in the past and continue now.
[learn]
## From the past until now
The situation **started in the past** and **is still true now**.
= I **have lived** here **for** ten years. (I came ten years ago; I still live here.)
= She **has known** him **since** 2015.
= We**'ve been** married **for** six months.

## How long…?
= **How long have** you **lived** in Chennai?
= **How long has** she **been** a teacher?
= **How long have** you **had** this car?

## Present perfect continuous: have been + -ing
For actions that continue (often with action verbs):
= I**'ve been waiting** for an hour!
= It**'s been raining** since this morning.
= How long **have** you **been learning** English?

> State verbs (know, be, have for possession, like) use the simple form: I've **known** her for years (not ~~I've been knowing~~).

## Present vs present perfect
| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| ~~I live here for five years.~~ | I**'ve lived** here for five years. |
| ~~I know her since 2010.~~ | I**'ve known** her since 2010. |
| ~~How long are you here?~~ | How long **have** you **been** here? |

The present simple (*I live here*) is only about now. When we say **how long until now**, use the present perfect.
[examples]
I've worked at this company for three years.
How long have you known your best friend?
They've lived in this house since 2012.
She's been ill since Monday.
We've been waiting for the doctor for forty minutes.
How long has it been snowing?
I've had this phone for two years.
He's been playing the guitar since he was a child.
[mistakes]
I am here since 9 o'clock. => I have been here since 9 o'clock. | I've been here since 9 o'clock. :: From past to now → present perfect.
I know him for years. => I have known him for years. | I've known him for years. :: Use have known.
How long you live here? => How long have you lived here? :: How long + have + subject + participle.
[easy]
mc: I ___ here since 2019. | live | *have lived | am living :: since → present perfect
mc: How long ___ you known Raj? | do | *have | are :: How long + have
fill: She ___ (be) a nurse for ten years. = has been | 's been :: from the past until now
fill: We ___ (have) this dog since 2020. = have had | 've had :: have → had
mc: They've been married ___ 25 years. | since | *for | from :: for + a period
order: I have known her for a long time. :: have + known + for
[medium]
fix: I live in this flat for six years. => I have lived in this flat for six years. | I've lived in this flat for six years. :: until now → present perfect
fill: It ___ (rain) since lunch time. = has been raining | 's been raining :: action continuing → has been + -ing
mc: How long ___ learning Spanish? | are you | *have you been | do you :: have you been + -ing
fill: How long ___ your parents ___ (be) married? = have, been :: have + been

# 19 | for, since, ago | Present perfect | A2
summary: for + a period (for two hours), since + a starting point (since Monday), ago = back from now with the past simple (two days ago).
[learn]
## for and since (often with the present perfect)
| **for** + a period of time | **since** + a starting point |
|---|---|
| for **ten minutes** | since **8 o'clock** |
| for **three days** | since **Monday** |
| for **six months** | since **April** |
| for **two years** | since **2015** |
| for **a long time** | since **I was a child** |

= I've been here **for** an hour. / I've been here **since** 9 o'clock.
= She's lived in Pune **for** five years. / … **since** 2020.

> *for* can be used with other tenses too: I stayed there **for** two weeks. *since* is used with the present perfect.

## ago = before now
**ago** goes **after** the time, with the **past simple**.
= I met her **two years ago**.
= The train left **ten minutes ago**.
= **How long ago** did you start this job?

## Compare
= I **started** this job three years **ago**. (past simple + ago)
= I**'ve had** this job **for** three years. (present perfect + for)
= I**'ve had** this job **since** 2022. (present perfect + since)
All three describe the same situation!
[examples]
We've been friends for twenty years.
It's been cold since the weekend.
I arrived here three hours ago.
He's worked here since he left university.
They moved to Delhi a month ago.
I haven't seen my cousin for ages. :: for ages = for a very long time
She's been a vegetarian since 2018.
How long ago did the film start?
[mistakes]
I've lived here since five years. => I've lived here for five years. :: Period → for.
I met him before two weeks. => I met him two weeks ago. :: Use ago after the time.
I've been here for 8 o'clock. => I've been here since 8 o'clock. :: Starting point → since.

fill: They've been married ___ ten years. = for :: period
order: The bus left five minutes ago. :: time + ago
[medium]
mc: I haven't eaten ___ breakfast. | for | *since | ago :: breakfast = starting point
fill: My grandfather died two years ___. = ago :: past simple + ago
fix: She has worked here since six months. => She has worked here for six months. | She's worked here for six months. :: period → for
mc: How long ___ did you buy this car? | for | since | *ago :: How long ago + past simple
fill: It's been raining ___ hours! = for :: period
order: I have had this watch since my birthday. :: since + starting point
[hard]
mc: Which means the same as "I started learning yoga a year ago"? | I learn yoga for a year. | *I've been learning yoga for a year. | I've learnt yoga since a year. :: ago → for + present perfect

# 20 | Present perfect (I have done) vs past simple (I did) | Present perfect | B1
summary: Use the present perfect when the time is not finished or not said; use the past simple with a finished time (yesterday, last week, in 2019, ago).
[learn]
## The main difference
| Present perfect | Past simple |
|---|---|
| connected to **now** | finished time, **not** connected to now |
| time not said, or unfinished time | finished time is said or clear |
| = I**'ve lost** my keys. (I don't have them now) | = I **lost** my keys yesterday. (maybe I found them) |
| = I**'ve been** to Rome. (in my life) | = I **went** to Rome **in 2019**. |

## Finished time words → past simple
**yesterday, last week, last year, in 2020, ago, when I was young, What time…? When…?**
= ✅ I **saw** him **yesterday**. ❌ ~~I've seen him yesterday.~~
= ✅ **When did** you **arrive**? ❌ ~~When have you arrived?~~

## Unfinished time → present perfect
**today, this week, this month, this year, so far, recently, ever, never, yet, just, already**
= I**'ve drunk** three coffees **today**. (today isn't finished)
= Have you seen any good films **recently**?

## Conversation pattern
Start with present perfect (news / experience) → details with past simple.
= "I**'ve had** an accident!" "Oh no! What **happened**?" "I **fell** off my bike this morning."

> **Mozart wrote** 600 pieces (not ~~has written~~), because Mozart is dead — the time is finished.
[examples]
I've finished the report. Here it is.
I finished the report at 6 o'clock yesterday.
Have you ever been to Egypt? — Yes, I went there in 2018.
She's had three cups of tea this morning. :: morning not finished yet
She had three cups of tea this morning. :: said in the afternoon → morning is finished
When did you start learning English?
We've lived here since 2015. Before that we lived in Bangalore.
Gandhi was born in 1869. :: finished time
[mistakes]
I have seen him yesterday. => I saw him yesterday. :: yesterday → past simple.
When have you arrived? => When did you arrive? :: When…? → past simple.
Did you ever see a ghost? => Have you ever seen a ghost? :: Experience in your life → present perfect.

fill: We ___ (go) to Mysore in 2021. = went :: in 2021 → past simple
fill: She ___ (not / call) me today. = hasn't called | has not called :: today → present perfect
order: I have never seen a tiger. :: experience → present perfect
[medium]
fix: I've met her two weeks ago. => I met her two weeks ago. :: ago → past simple
mc: When ___ the film start? | has | *did | have :: When → past simple
fill: ___ you ___ (hear) the news? The president is visiting our city! = Have, heard :: news → present perfect
fill: I ___ (live) in Chennai for five years, but then I moved. = lived :: finished period

mc: Which is correct? | I've finished school in 2015. | *I finished school in 2015. | I've finish school in 2015. :: in 2015 → past simple

# 21 | Passive: is done, was done | Passive | A2
summary: The passive (am/is/are/was/were + past participle) focuses on the thing that receives the action: Rice is grown in India. The bridge was built in 1990.
[learn]
## Active and passive
| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| Someone **cleans** the office every day. | The office **is cleaned** every day. |
| Somebody **built** this house in 1950. | This house **was built** in 1950. |

We use the passive when **what happens** is more important than **who** does it, or when we don't know who did it.

## Present simple passive: am / is / are + past participle
= English **is spoken** all over the world.
= These phones **are made** in China.
= The rooms **are cleaned** every morning.

## Past simple passive: was / were + past participle
= My bike **was stolen** last night.
= The windows **were broken** in the storm.
= When **was** the Taj Mahal **built**?

## by…
Use **by** to say who did it (only if it is important):
= Romeo and Juliet **was written by** Shakespeare.
= The telephone **was invented by** Bell.

## Negatives and questions
= Breakfast **isn't included** in the price.
= **Is** this room **used** much?
= **Were** you **invited** to the wedding?
= I **wasn't told** about the meeting.

> I **was born** in… is a passive form too.
[examples]
Tea is grown in Assam and Darjeeling.
This bridge was built in 1932.
The museum is visited by thousands of people every year.
Our car was stolen last week.
Are credit cards accepted here?
The match wasn't shown on TV.
The Harry Potter books were written by J.K. Rowling.
Cheese is made from milk.
[mistakes]
The window broke by my son. => The window was broken by my son. :: Passive needs be + participle.
English is speak in many countries. => English is spoken in many countries. :: Use the past participle.
This house built in 1920. => This house was built in 1920. :: Don't forget was.

order: The office is cleaned every evening. :: is + participle
[medium]

fix: The windows was washed yesterday. => The windows were washed yesterday. :: plural → were

# 22 | Passive: is being done, has been done | Passive | B1
summary: Continuous and perfect passives: My car is being repaired (now). The room has been painted (result now).
[learn]
## Present continuous passive: am / is / are + being + participle
Something is happening **now** to the subject.
= My car **is being repaired**. (Someone is repairing it now.)
= The bridge **is being painted** this month.
= Sorry, this room **is being used** at the moment.

## Present perfect passive: have / has + been + participle
Something happened, and there is a result **now**.
= My car **has been repaired**. (It's ready now.)
= The match **has been cancelled** because of the rain.
= Two people **have been arrested**.

## Compare
| | |
|---|---|
| The room **is cleaned** every day. | (habit — present simple passive) |
| The room **is being cleaned**. | (now — you can't go in) |
| The room **has been cleaned**. | (finished — it's clean now) |
| The room **was cleaned** yesterday. | (finished time — past simple passive) |

## Negatives and questions
= The email **hasn't been sent** yet.
= **Has** the parcel **been delivered**?
= **Is** anything **being done** about the noise?
[examples]
A new metro line is being built in our city.
The results have been published online.
I think we're being followed!
My bag hasn't been found yet.
Has the bill been paid?
The road is being repaired, so we have to go another way.
Your order has been sent.
The flight has been delayed by two hours.
[mistakes]
The house is building now. => The house is being built now. :: Passive continuous → is being + participle.
The letter has sent. => The letter has been sent. :: Perfect passive → has been + participle.
My car has been repair. => My car has been repaired. :: Use the past participle.

# 23 | be, have and do in present and past | Verb forms | A2
summary: be, have and do are both main verbs and helping (auxiliary) verbs. Learn their forms and how they make tenses, negatives and questions.
[learn]
## Their forms
| Verb | Present | Past | Participle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **be** | am / is / are | was / were | been |
| **have** | have / has | had | had |
| **do** | do / does | did | done |

## As helping verbs (auxiliaries)
| Tense | Helping verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| present continuous | **be** + -ing | She **is** working. |
| past continuous | **was/were** + -ing | We **were** sleeping. |
| passive | **be** + participle | It **was** made in Italy. |
| present perfect | **have** + participle | I **have** finished. |
| present simple neg/questions | **do/does** + base | **Do** you know? He **doesn't** care. |
| past simple neg/questions | **did** + base | **Did** you go? I **didn't** see. |

## As main verbs
= I **am** a student. (be = main verb)
= She **has** a sister. (have = main verb)
= I **do** yoga every morning. (do = main verb)

They can be main verb AND helping verb in one sentence:
= **Do** you **do** your homework every day?
= I **have had** a headache since noon.
= **Is** he **being** silly again?

## Which verb makes the negative / question?
The **helping verb** moves or takes *not*:
= She **is** reading. → **Is** she reading? / She **isn't** reading.
= They **have** left. → **Have** they left? / They **haven't** left.
= You **like** it. → **Do** you like it? / You **don't** like it.
[examples]
What are you doing? — I'm doing my homework.
Did you do the shopping? — No, I didn't have time.
Has she had her lunch yet?
They were waiting for us when we arrived.
I don't have any money.
The cake was made by my aunt.
What does your father do? — He's an engineer.
I haven't done anything wrong!
[mistakes]
What you are doing? => What are you doing? :: Helping verb before the subject in questions.
Did you did your homework? => Did you do your homework? :: did + base verb (do).
She hasn't any money. => She doesn't have any money. :: have (possess) → doesn't have.

# 24 | Regular and irregular verbs | Verb forms | A2
summary: Regular verbs add -ed for the past and past participle (work–worked–worked). Irregular verbs have special forms (go–went–gone).
[learn]
## Regular verbs
The past simple and past participle both end in **-ed**:
| Base | Past simple | Past participle |
|---|---|---|
| work | work**ed** | work**ed** |
| live | live**d** | live**d** |
| study | stud**ied** | stud**ied** |
| stop | sto**pped** | sto**pped** |

## Irregular verbs
The past simple and/or participle are not -ed. Some patterns:
| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| all three the same | cut–cut–cut, put–put–put, hit–hit–hit |
| past = participle | buy–bought–bought, make–made–made, find–found–found |
| all three different | go–went–gone, see–saw–seen, write–wrote–written |
| base = participle | come–came–come, run–ran–run, become–became–become |

## Where do we use each form?
| Form | Used in | Example |
|---|---|---|
| past simple | finished past | I **saw** her yesterday. |
| past participle | present perfect | I've **seen** this film. |
| past participle | passive | It was **seen** by millions. |

> Learn the verbs in groups. Open **Practice → Irregular verbs → Groups** for flashcards and a typing drill.
[examples]
I walked to work and Mina drove. :: regular / irregular
We've eaten here before. :: eat → eaten
She told me a secret. :: tell → told
The window was broken by the wind. :: break → broken
They put the books on the shelf. :: put → put → put
Have you ever ridden a camel? :: ride → ridden
I found 100 rupees in my old jeans.
He has become a famous chef. :: become → became → become
[mistakes]

She has wrote a book. => She has written a book. | She's written a book. :: write → written
He buyed a new phone. => He bought a new phone. :: buy → bought
