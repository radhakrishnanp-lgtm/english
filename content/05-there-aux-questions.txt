## Use only the auxiliary to avoid repeating
= I'm not hungry, but my brother **is**. (= is hungry)
= She can swim, but I **can't**. (= can't swim)
= "Have you finished?" "Yes, I **have**."
= He likes tea, but I **don't**.

| Main sentence | Auxiliary |
|---|---|

| past simple (went, saw) | **did / didn't** |
| present perfect (have seen) | **have / has / haven't / hasn't** |
| modal (can, will, should…) | the same **modal** |

= "Are you ready?" "Yes, I **am**." / "No, I**'m not**."

## Direct and indirect questions
An indirect question is inside another sentence, like *Do you know…?*, *Can you tell me…?*, *I don't know…*, *I wonder…*.

After the question word, use **normal word order** (subject + verb), **no do/does/did**.
| Direct question | Indirect question |
|---|---|
| Where **is** the station? | Do you know where **the station is**? |
| What time **is** it? | Can you tell me what time **it is**? |
| Where **does** she **live**? | I don't know where **she lives**. |
| Why **did** he **leave**? | I wonder why **he left**. |
| How much **does** it **cost**? | Do you know how much **it costs**? |

## Yes/No questions → if or whether
= **Is** she at home? → Do you know **if** she is at home?
= **Did** he call? → I don't know **whether** he called.

## Why use them?
They are more **polite**, especially with strangers:
= Excuse me, **could you tell me where the post office is**?

> The question mark goes at the end only if the whole sentence is a question: *Do you know where he is?* but *I don't know where he is.*
