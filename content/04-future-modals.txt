summary: have to + base verb = it is necessary (because of a rule or situation). Past: had to. Negative: don't have to = it isn't necessary.
[learn]
## Form
| | |
|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | **have to** work |

| past (all) | **had to** work |

# 36 | I used to… | Modals, imperative etc. | A2
summary: used to + base verb = something that was true or happened regularly in the past, but not now: I used to live in Delhi. He used to smoke.
[learn]
## used to + base verb (past habits and states)
= I **used to** play cricket every day. (but I don't now)
= She **used to** have long hair. (now it's short)
= There **used to** be a cinema here. (now there isn't)

*Used to* is the **same for all subjects** and only refers to the **past**.

## Negative and questions: didn't use to / Did … use to?
In negatives and questions, write **use to** (no -d) after *did*:
= I **didn't use to** like vegetables. (now I do)
= **Did** you **use to** walk to school?

## Compare with the present
| Past (used to) | Now (present simple) |
|---|---|
| I **used to** drink milk. | I **drink** coffee now. |
| He **used to** live in a village. | He **lives** in the city now. |

> There is no present form: ~~I use to get up at 6~~ → I **usually** get up at 6.

## used to or past simple?
For a single action, or when we say exactly how many times, use the **past simple**:
= I **went** to Ooty in 2019. (once)
= I **used to** go to Ooty every summer. (regularly)
[examples]

There used to be a big tree here.

She used to work in a bank. Now she's a teacher.
This town used to be very quiet.
[mistakes]
I use to play football. => I used to play football. :: Past → used to.
Did you used to live here? => Did you use to live here? :: After did → use to.
I usually used to get up at 6 now. => I usually get up at 6. :: Present habit → present simple.
