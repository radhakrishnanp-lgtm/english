# 85 | old, nice, interesting (adjectives) | Adjectives and adverbs | A1
summary: Adjectives describe nouns. They go before a noun (a red car) or after be/look/feel/seem (The car is red). They never take -s.
[learn]
## Position
**adjective + noun**
= a **big** house · an **old** man · **beautiful** flowers
**be / look / feel / seem / sound / taste / smell / get / become + adjective**
= The house is **big**.
= You look **tired**.
= This soup tastes **delicious**.
= I feel **happy** today.

## No plural form
= a **nice** day → two **nice** days (not ~~nices days~~)

## Two or more adjectives — usual order
**opinion → size → age → shape → colour → origin → material → noun**
= a **lovely little old** house
= a **big black** dog
= a **beautiful Indian silk** sari

## -ed and -ing adjectives
| -ed = how you feel | -ing = what causes the feeling |
|---|---|
| I'm **bored**. | The film is **boring**. |
| She was **interested**. | The book is **interesting**. |
| We were **tired**. | The journey was **tiring**. |
| I'm **excited**. | It's **exciting** news! |
[examples]
She has long black hair.
This is a really interesting book.
I'm tired after my long journey.
The coffee smells wonderful.
They live in a small white house.
We had a lovely time.
The lesson was boring, so I was bored.
My grandmother has a beautiful old wooden box.
[mistakes]
I have a car red. => I have a red car. :: Adjective before the noun.
They are bigs houses. => They are big houses. :: Adjectives have no plural.
I'm very boring in this class. => I'm very bored in this class. :: How you feel → -ed.
[easy]
mc: She has a ___. | car new | *new car | cars new :: adjective + noun
mc: These are ___ flowers. | beautifuls | *beautiful | beautifully :: no plural
fill: This film is very ___ (interest). = interesting :: the film causes the feeling → -ing
fill: I feel ___ (tire). I need to sleep. = tired :: how you feel → -ed
mc: The food tastes ___. | well | *good | goodly :: taste + adjective
order: He lives in a big old house. :: size + age + noun
[medium]
fix: She bought a dress blue. => She bought a blue dress. :: adjective before the noun
mc: I was ___ by the end of the lesson. | boring | *bored | bore :: how I felt → -ed
fill: The news was very ___ (surprise). = surprising :: news causes the feeling
mc: Which order is correct? | a black big dog | *a big black dog | a dog big black :: size before colour
fill: You look ___ (worry). Is everything OK? = worried :: look + -ed adjective
order: We stayed in a lovely small hotel. :: opinion + size + noun
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | an old lovely wooden table | *a lovely old wooden table | a wooden lovely old table :: opinion → age → material
fix: The match was very excited. => The match was very exciting. :: the match causes the feeling
fill: I'm ___ (interest) in photography. = interested :: how I feel
mc: She wore a ___ dress. | silk red long | *long red silk | red long silk :: size → colour → material
fix: These shoes are comfortables. => These shoes are comfortable. :: no -s on adjectives
fill: That's a really ___ (annoy) noise! = annoying :: the noise causes the feeling

# 86 | quickly, badly, suddenly (adverbs) | Adjectives and adverbs | A2
summary: Adverbs describe verbs — how something happens: She speaks slowly. Most adverbs = adjective + -ly. Irregular: good → well, fast → fast, hard → hard.
[learn]
## Adjective vs adverb
| Adjective (describes a noun) | Adverb (describes a verb) |
|---|---|
| He's a **careful** driver. | He drives **carefully**. |
| She's a **quick** learner. | She learns **quickly**. |
| It was a **bad** game. | They played **badly**. |

## Spelling
| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| + **-ly** | slow → slow**ly**, quiet → quiet**ly** |
| -y → **-ily** | easy → eas**ily**, happy → happ**ily** |
| -le → **-ly** | terrible → terrib**ly**, gentle → gent**ly** |
| -ic → **-ically** | automatic → automat**ically** |

## Irregular adverbs
| Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|
| good | **well** |
| fast | **fast** |
| hard | **hard** (not ~~hardly~~ — that means "almost not") |
| late | **late** |
| early | **early** |

= She speaks English very **well**. (not ~~very good~~)
= He works **hard**.

## After be/look/feel/seem → adjective (not adverb)
= You look **happy**. (not ~~happily~~)
= I feel **good**. / I'm **well**. (well = healthy)
[examples]
Please drive carefully.
She sings beautifully.
He answered the question correctly.
The children played happily in the garden.
You speak English very well.
I got up early this morning.
The train arrived late.
Suddenly, the lights went out.
[mistakes]
He drives very careful. => He drives very carefully. :: Describes the verb → adverb.
She speaks English very good. => She speaks English very well. :: good → well.
He works hardly. => He works hard. :: hard is already an adverb.
[easy]
mc: Please speak ___. | slow | *slowly | slowness :: describes "speak" → adverb
mc: She plays the piano very ___. | good | *well | goodly :: good → well
fill: He ran ___ (quick) to catch the bus. = quickly :: adverb
fill: The children are playing ___ (quiet). = quietly :: adverb
mc: He's a ___ driver. | carefully | *careful | care :: describes a noun → adjective
order: She answered all the questions easily. :: verb + adverb
[medium]
fix: You did the work very good. => You did the work very well. :: adverb → well
mc: I work ___ every day. | hardly | *hard | hardy :: hard (adverb) = with effort
fill: The team played ___ (terrible). = terribly :: -le → -ly
mc: You look ___ today. | happily | *happy | happiness :: look + adjective
fill: The door opened ___ (automatic). = automatically :: -ic → -ically
order: The bus arrived late again. :: late = adverb
[hard]
mc: "I can hardly see" means: | I can see well. | *I can almost not see. | I can see hard. :: hardly = almost not
fix: She dances beautiful. => She dances beautifully. :: adverb
fill: The soup smells ___ (wonderful). = wonderful :: smell + adjective
mc: Which is correct? | He spoke angry. | *He spoke angrily. | He spoke angrier. :: adverb describes "spoke"
fix: Drive slow! There are children here. => Drive slowly! There are children here. :: adverb
fill: "How are you?" "I'm very ___, thanks." (healthy) = well :: well = healthy

# 87 | old/older, expensive/more expensive (comparatives) | Adjectives and adverbs | A2
summary: Comparatives compare two things: short adjectives add -er (older, bigger), long adjectives use more (more expensive). Irregular: good → better, bad → worse, far → further.
[learn]
## Short adjectives (1 syllable): + -er
| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| + **-er** | old → old**er**, cheap → cheap**er** |
| -e → + **-r** | nice → nice**r**, late → late**r** |
| C-V-C → double | big → bi**gg**er, hot → ho**tt**er, thin → thi**nn**er |
| -y → **-ier** (2 syllables) | easy → eas**ier**, happy → happ**ier**, busy → bus**ier** |

## Long adjectives (2+ syllables): more + adjective
= **more expensive**, **more beautiful**, **more interesting**, **more difficult**, **more comfortable**

## Irregular
| | |
|---|---|
| good / well | **better** |
| bad / badly | **worse** |
| far | **further** / farther |

## Adverbs
= **more slowly**, **more carefully**; but **faster**, **harder**, **earlier**

## much / a bit + comparative
= This hotel is **much bigger**.
= Can you speak **a bit slower**?
= I feel **much better** today.

> Never use both: ~~more bigger~~, ~~more better~~.
[examples]
My brother is older than me.
Delhi is bigger than Mysore.
This phone is more expensive than that one.
Today is hotter than yesterday.
English is easier than Chinese for me.
I'm feeling much better now.
The weather is worse today.
Could you speak more slowly, please?
[mistakes]
This is more cheaper. => This is cheaper. :: Don't use more with -er.
She is more tall than me. => She is taller than me. :: Short adjective → -er.
My English is gooder now. => My English is better now. :: good → better.
[easy]
mc: An elephant is ___ than a horse. | big | *bigger | more big :: big → bigger
mc: Gold is ___ than silver. | expensiver | *more expensive | more expensiver :: long → more
mc: My cold is ___ today. I feel great. | gooder | *better | more good :: good → better
fill: Summer is ___ (hot) than winter. = hotter :: double t
fill: This test is ___ (easy) than the last one. = easier :: y → ier
order: My sister is younger than me. :: comparative + than
[medium]
fix: The second film was more better. => The second film was better. :: no more + better
mc: The traffic is ___ today than yesterday. | bad | *worse | badder :: bad → worse
fill: This chair is ___ (comfortable) than that one. = more comfortable :: long → more
fill: Can you walk a bit ___ (fast)? = faster :: fast → faster
mc: Rome is much ___ from here than Paris. | far | *further | more far :: far → further
order: The new flat is much bigger. :: much + comparative
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | He drives more careful than me. | *He drives more carefully than me. | He drives carefullier than me. :: adverb → more carefully
fix: My room is more tidier than yours. => My room is tidier than yours. :: no double comparative
fill: Life in the city is ___ (busy) than in the village. = busier :: y → ier
fill: Your English is getting ___ and ___ (good). = better, better :: better and better
mc: The exam was ___ than I expected. | difficulter | *more difficult | most difficult :: long → more
fix: She runs more fast than her brother. => She runs faster than her brother. :: fast → faster

# 88 | older than…, more expensive than… | Adjectives and adverbs | A2
summary: Use than after a comparative: She's taller than me / than I am. Use not as … as for the opposite (see Unit 89).
[learn]
## comparative + than
= My house is **bigger than** yours.
= Trains are **faster than** buses.
= She is **more confident than** her sister.

## than me / than I am
= He's taller **than me**. (normal spoken English)
= He's taller **than I am**. (also correct)
= ❌ ~~than I~~ (sounds very formal)

## than + clause
= It's **colder than** I thought.
= The film was **better than** we expected.
= You're **later than** usual.

## How much bigger? much / a lot / far / a bit / a little / slightly
= Gold is **much more expensive than** silver.
= I'm **a bit older than** my wife.
= The new road is **far better than** the old one.

## more / less + noun + than
= I have **more** books **than** you.
= He earns **less** money **than** his brother.
= There are **fewer** people here **than** yesterday.
[examples]
The Ganges is longer than the Yamuna.
This bag is cheaper than that one.
My father is much stronger than me.
Flying is more expensive than going by train.
The exam was easier than I expected.
I get up earlier than my husband.
He has more friends than I do.
Today is a bit warmer than yesterday.
[mistakes]
She is taller then me. => She is taller than me. :: than (not then).
My car is faster that yours. => My car is faster than yours. :: than
He is more older than me. => He is older than me. :: no more with -er.

mc: Tea is ___ than coffee in this café. | cheap | *cheaper | more cheap :: cheaper than
order: My bag is heavier than yours. :: comparative + than
[medium]

order: The new phone is a lot more expensive. :: a lot + comparative
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | There are less cars than before. | *There are fewer cars than before. | There are few cars than before. :: countable → fewer
fill: It's ___ (cold) today than it was yesterday. = colder :: comparative
fix: He earns more money that me. => He earns more money than me. | He earns more money than I do. :: than
fill: This sofa is far ___ (comfortable) than the old one. = more comfortable :: far + more + adjective
mc: You're ___ than usual today. Is everything OK? | quiet | *quieter | more quieter :: comparative
fix: Your English is more good than mine. => Your English is better than mine. :: good → better

# 89 | not as … as | Adjectives and adverbs | B1
summary: as + adjective + as = the same (She's as tall as me). not as … as = less (A bike is not as fast as a car).
[learn]
## not as … as = less than
= A bike is **not as fast as** a car. (= a car is faster)
= The film was**n't as good as** the book.
= I'm **not as tall as** my brother.

## as … as = the same
= Ravi is **as tall as** his father. (they are the same height)
= This phone is **as expensive as** a laptop!
= Come **as soon as** you can.

## not as … as = less … than
| | |
|---|---|
| Delhi is **not as big as** Mumbai. | = Delhi is **smaller than** Mumbai. |
| Hindi is **not as difficult as** Chinese. | = Hindi is **less difficult than** Chinese. |

## as much / as many
= I don't have **as much** money **as** you.
= She doesn't have **as many** friends **as** her sister.

## the same as
= Your phone is **the same as** mine.
= My shoes are **the same colour as** yours.

> After *as*, use **me / him / her** (normal) or **I am / he is**: She's as old as **me** / as **I am**.
[examples]
Silver isn't as expensive as gold.
My son is almost as tall as me.
The second hotel wasn't as nice as the first.
I can't run as fast as you.
Is Pune as hot as Chennai?
Please call me as soon as possible.
She doesn't eat as much as her brother.
His car is the same as mine.
[mistakes]
She is as tall than me. => She is as tall as me. :: as … as
It's not as cheaper as I thought. => It's not as cheap as I thought. :: as + normal adjective.
My bag is the same like yours. => My bag is the same as yours. :: the same as
[easy]
mc: A cat isn't as big ___ a dog. | than | *as | like :: as … as
mc: My phone is ___ as yours. | same | *the same | as same :: the same as
fill: I'm not as tall ___ my father. = as :: not as … as
fill: Your bag is as heavy ___ mine. = as :: as … as
mc: Silver is not as ___ as gold. | expensiver | *expensive | more expensive :: normal adjective
order: The town isn't as busy as the city. :: not as … as
[medium]
fix: This book isn't as interesting than the film. => This book isn't as interesting as the film. :: as … as
mc: "Delhi isn't as big as Mumbai" means: | Delhi is bigger. | *Mumbai is bigger. | They are the same. :: not as … as = less
fill: She doesn't have as ___ free time as me. = much :: uncountable → as much
fill: Please reply as ___ as possible. = soon :: as soon as possible
mc: He doesn't have as ___ books as I do. | much | *many | more :: countable → as many
order: It wasn't as cold as yesterday. :: not as … as
[hard]
fix: My coffee is the same like yours. => My coffee is the same as yours. :: the same as
fill: "Train is faster than bus" → A bus is not ___ fast ___ a train. = as, as :: not as … as
mc: Which means "the same"? | not as old as | *as old as | older than :: as … as = equal
fix: He's not so clever like his brother. => He's not as clever as his brother. | He's not so clever as his brother. :: not as … as
fill: My English is ___ good as yours now! = as :: equal
mc: I can't swim as ___ as you. | good | *well | better :: swim → adverb well

# 90 | the oldest, the most expensive (superlatives) | Adjectives and adverbs | A2
summary: Superlatives compare one thing with the whole group: the oldest, the biggest, the most expensive, the best, the worst. Use in/of after them.
[learn]
## Short adjectives: the + -est
| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| + **-est** | old → **the** old**est**, cheap → **the** cheap**est** |
| -e → + **-st** | nice → **the** nice**st** |
| C-V-C → double | big → **the** bi**gg**est, hot → **the** ho**tt**est |
| -y → **-iest** | happy → **the** happ**iest**, easy → **the** eas**iest** |

## Long adjectives: the most + adjective
= **the most expensive**, **the most beautiful**, **the most difficult**

## Irregular
| | | |
|---|---|---|
| good | better | **the best** |
| bad | worse | **the worst** |
| far | further | **the furthest** |

## in / of
- **in** + a place or group: the tallest building **in** the world, the best student **in** the class
- **of** + a period / number: the happiest day **of** my life, the oldest **of** the three

## Present perfect + ever
= It's **the best** film I've **ever** seen.
= She's **the kindest** person I've **ever** met.

## one of the + superlative + plural
= Delhi is **one of the biggest cities** in India.
[examples]
Everest is the highest mountain in the world.
This is the most expensive restaurant in town.
He's the funniest person I know.
It was the worst day of my life!
She's the best player in the team.
What's the longest river in India?
That was the most interesting lesson of the week.
Mumbai is one of the busiest cities in the world.
[mistakes]
He's the most tall boy in the class. => He's the tallest boy in the class. :: Short → -est.
It's the better film I've seen. => It's the best film I've seen. :: good → the best.
She's the cleverest of the class. => She's the cleverest in the class. :: group/place → in.
[easy]
mc: The cheetah is the ___ animal. | fast | faster | *fastest :: superlative → -est
mc: This is the ___ book I've read. | good | better | *best :: good → best
fill: Today is the ___ (hot) day of the year. = hottest :: double t
fill: This is the most ___ (beautiful) beach in Goa. = beautiful :: the most + adjective
mc: It was the ___ day of my life. | worse | *worst | baddest :: bad → worst
order: She is the youngest in her family. :: the + -est + in
[medium]
fix: This is the most cheap hotel. => This is the cheapest hotel. :: short → -est
mc: He's the tallest student ___ the school. | of | *in | at :: place/group → in
fill: It's the ___ (funny) film I've ever seen. = funniest :: y → iest
mc: Russia is ___ country in the world. | the larger | *the largest | largest :: the + -est
fill: This is one of the ___ (difficult) exams in the country. = most difficult :: one of the + most
order: That was the best meal I've ever had. :: superlative + present perfect + ever
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | Mumbai is one of the biggest city in India. | *Mumbai is one of the biggest cities in India. | Mumbai is one of biggest cities in India. :: one of the + plural
fix: She is the most happiest person I know. => She is the happiest person I know. :: no double superlative
fill: Of the three brothers, Arun is the ___ (old). = oldest | eldest :: the oldest of
fix: It's the more beautiful place I've ever visited. => It's the most beautiful place I've ever visited. :: superlative → most
mc: Of all the planets, Neptune is the ___ from the sun. | farer | *furthest | most far :: far → the furthest
fill: What's the ___ (bad) mistake you've ever made? = worst :: bad → worst

# 91 | enough | Adjectives and adverbs | B1
summary: adjective/adverb + enough (old enough, fast enough). enough + noun (enough money, enough time). Not enough = less than we need.
[learn]
## enough + noun
= We have **enough food** for everyone.
= I don't have **enough money** to buy a car.
= Are there **enough chairs**?

## adjective / adverb + enough
**enough** goes **after** an adjective or adverb:
= She's **old enough** to drive. (not ~~enough old~~)
= The room isn't **big enough**.
= You're not running **fast enough**!

## enough without a noun
= "Do you want more rice?" "No thanks, I've had **enough**."
= Ten euros isn't **enough**.

## enough + for someone / to + verb
= Is this coffee **hot enough for you**?
= He isn't **tall enough to reach** the shelf.
= There isn't **enough time to finish**.

| Position | Example |
|---|---|
| enough + **noun** | enough **money** |
| **adjective** + enough | **warm** enough |
| **adverb** + enough | **quickly** enough |
[examples]
Is there enough milk for my coffee?
I'm not tall enough to play basketball.
This bag isn't big enough for all my things.
We didn't have enough time to visit the museum.
He's old enough to make his own decisions.
Stop! I've heard enough.
You didn't study hard enough.
Are you warm enough?
[mistakes]
She isn't enough old to vote. => She isn't old enough to vote. :: adjective + enough.
We don't have money enough. => We don't have enough money. :: enough + noun.
I'm not strong enough for lift this. => I'm not strong enough to lift this. :: enough + to + verb.
[easy]
mc: Is the water ___ for swimming? | enough warm | *warm enough | warm enoughly :: adjective + enough
mc: We don't have ___. | chairs enough | *enough chairs | enough of chairs :: enough + noun
fill: He's old ___ to drive a car. = enough :: adjective + enough
fill: There isn't ___ time to cook. = enough :: enough + noun
mc: "More cake?" "No thanks, I've had ___." | enough of | *enough | enoughly :: alone → enough
order: This room isn't big enough. :: adjective + enough
[medium]
fix: He doesn't speak enough clearly. => He doesn't speak clearly enough. :: adverb + enough
mc: I'm not tall enough ___ the top shelf. | for reach | *to reach | reaching :: enough + to + verb
fill: Is the soup hot enough ___ you? = for :: enough for someone
fix: We have bread enough. => We have enough bread. :: enough + noun
mc: You didn't work ___. | enough hard | *hard enough | enough hardly :: adverb + enough
order: Do you have enough money for the ticket? :: enough + noun + for
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | The box isn't enough strong. | *The box isn't strong enough. | The box isn't strongly enough. :: adjective + enough
fill: The film was good, but not good ___ to watch twice. = enough :: adjective + enough + to
fix: She isn't enough experienced for this job. => She isn't experienced enough for this job. :: adjective + enough
fill: There weren't ___ seats for everybody, so some people stood. = enough :: enough + noun
mc: He ran ___ to catch the train. | enough fast | *fast enough | fastly enough :: adverb + enough
fix: I didn't sleep enough well last night. => I didn't sleep well enough last night. :: adverb + enough

# 92 | too | Adjectives and adverbs | B1
summary: too + adjective/adverb = more than is good or possible (too hot, too fast). too much + uncountable, too many + plural. Compare with not … enough.
[learn]
## too + adjective / adverb
**too** = more than is good / more than we want:
= This coffee is **too hot**. I can't drink it.
= You're driving **too fast**!
= The shoes are **too small** for me.

## too much / too many + noun
= There's **too much** sugar in this tea.
= There are **too many** people here.
= I ate **too much**. (no noun)

## too … for someone / to + verb
= This bag is **too heavy for me**.
= She's **too young to drive**.
= It's **too late to call** him now.

## too vs very
| | |
|---|---|
| It's **very** hot, but I can drink it. | very = a lot (no problem) |
| It's **too** hot. I can't drink it. | too = a problem |

## too vs not … enough
= The box is **too heavy**. = The box is **not light enough**.
= He's **too short** to reach it. = He's **not tall enough** to reach it.
[examples]
It's too cold to go swimming today.
This shirt is too big for me.
You work too much. You need a holiday.
There are too many cars in the city centre.
I was too tired to cook dinner.
The music is too loud. Turn it down!
It's too expensive. I can't buy it.
He spoke too quickly and I didn't understand.
[mistakes]
It's too much hot. => It's too hot. :: too + adjective (no much).
There are too much people. => There are too many people. :: plural → too many.
The tea is too hot for drink. => The tea is too hot to drink. :: too + adjective + to + verb.
[easy]
mc: I can't wear these jeans. They're ___ small. | very | *too | enough :: problem → too
mc: There are ___ cars in this street. | too much | *too many | too :: plural → too many
mc: There's ___ salt in the soup. | too many | *too much | too :: uncountable → too much
fill: She's ___ young to watch this film. = too :: too + adjective + to
fill: The music is too ___ (loud). Turn it down. = loud :: too + adjective
order: It's too late to go out. :: too + adjective + to
[medium]
fix: I'm too much busy today. => I'm too busy today. :: too + adjective
mc: The bag is ___ heavy, but I can carry it. | too | *very | enough :: no problem → very
fill: This box is too heavy ___ me. = for :: too … for someone
mc: The box is too heavy = The box is not ___ enough. | heavy | *light | lighter :: opposite with not … enough
fill: You eat too ___ chocolate! = much :: uncountable
order: He drives too fast. :: too + adverb
[hard]
mc: Which shows a problem? | The soup is very hot, but it's delicious. | *The soup is too hot to eat. | The soup is hot enough. :: too = problem
fix: It's too dark for see anything. => It's too dark to see anything. :: too + adjective + to + verb
fill: There were too ___ questions in the exam. I didn't finish. = many :: countable plural
fix: She's too much tired to walk. => She's too tired to walk. :: too + adjective
mc: "He's too short to be a pilot." = He's not ___ to be a pilot. | short enough | *tall enough | enough tall :: not + opposite + enough
fill: I can't sleep. It's too ___ (noise) here. = noisy :: too + adjective

# 93 | He speaks English very well (word order 1) | Word order | A2
summary: Basic word order: subject + verb + object. Don't put anything between the verb and its object: She speaks English well (not She speaks well English). Place → time.
[learn]
## Subject + verb + object
= **I** (subject) **like** (verb) **coffee** (object).
= **She** **cooked** **dinner**.

## Verb + object stay together
Don't put adverbs between the verb and the object:
| ✅ | ❌ |
|---|---|
| She speaks **English** very well. | ~~She speaks very well English.~~ |
| I like **cricket** very much. | ~~I like very much cricket.~~ |

# 95 | still, yet, already | Word order | B1
summary: still = continuing (I'm still hungry). yet = until now, in negatives and questions (I haven't finished yet). already = sooner than expected (I've already eaten).
[learn]
## still = something continues, it hasn't stopped
= It's 11 pm and he's **still** working.
= Do you **still** live in Kochi?
= I **still** don't understand. (negative: still + don't)
Position: before the main verb, after *be*.

## yet = until now (negatives and questions, at the end)
= I haven't finished **yet**.
= Has the bus come **yet**?
= "Are you ready?" "Not **yet**."

## already = before now / sooner than expected
= "Do you want lunch?" "No, I've **already** eaten."
= It's only 9 am and she's **already** tired.
= Have you **already** paid? (surprise)

## Compare
| | |
|---|---|
| She's **still** asleep. | she hasn't woken up — it continues |
| She hasn't woken up **yet**. | until now, not happened |
| She's **already** awake. | sooner than expected |

## still vs any more
= He **still** lives here. (continues) ↔ He doesn't live here **any more**. (stopped)
[examples]
Is it still raining?
I've already told you three times!
Have you found a flat yet?
My grandfather still works in his garden every day.
The film hasn't started yet.
We're still waiting for an answer.
She's already finished her homework.
I still haven't heard from him.
[mistakes]
I have still not finished. => I still haven't finished. | I haven't finished yet. :: still before haven't; or yet at the end.
I've finished yet. => I've already finished. :: Positive → already.
Do you live still here? => Do you still live here? :: still before the main verb.
[easy]

mc: I haven't eaten ___. | still | *yet | already :: negative → yet
mc: "Lunch?" "No thanks, I've ___ eaten." | yet | still | *already :: before now → already
fill: Has the post arrived ___? = yet :: question → yet
fill: Do you ___ play the guitar? = still :: continues
order: The baby is still sleeping. :: still after be
[medium]
fix: Is he yet at work? => Is he still at work? :: continues → still
mc: It's only 10 am and he's ___ had three coffees! | yet | still | *already :: sooner than expected
fill: I ___ don't know the answer. = still :: still + negative
mc: "Have you finished?" "Not ___." | still | *yet | already :: not yet
fill: He doesn't work here any ___. = more :: stopped → any more
order: I still haven't booked the tickets. :: still + haven't

# 96 | Give me that book! Give it to me! | Word order | B1
summary: With give, send, show, lend etc.: give + person + thing (Give me the book) or give + thing + to + person (Give the book to me). With it/them use the second form: Give it to me.
[learn]
## Two patterns
| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| verb + **person** + **thing** | I gave **Ana** **a present**. |
| verb + **thing** + **to** + **person** | I gave **a present** **to Ana**. |

Verbs: **give, send, show, lend, sell, pass, bring, offer, pay, teach, tell, write**
= Can you **pass me the salt**? / Can you **pass the salt to me**?
= She **sent her mother a card**. / She **sent a card to her mother**.

## With *it* or *them* → thing + to + person
= ✅ Give **it to me**. ❌ ~~Give me it.~~ (unusual)
= ✅ I lent **them to my friend**.
= ✅ Show **it to her**.

## for with buy, make, get, cook, find
= I **bought a gift for my sister**. / I **bought my sister a gift**.
= Can you **get some milk for me**?

## Verbs that need *to* (not person first)
**explain, say, describe, suggest**:
= ✅ Can you **explain the rule to me**? ❌ ~~explain me the rule~~
