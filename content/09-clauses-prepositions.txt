# 97 | and, but, or, so, because | Conjunctions and clauses | A1
summary: Join ideas with and (add), but (contrast), or (choice), so (result) and because (reason).
[learn]
## The five linking words
| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **and** | add information | I like tea **and** coffee. |
| **but** | contrast / surprise | It's cheap **but** good. |
| **or** | choice | Tea **or** coffee? |
| **so** | result | It was raining, **so** we stayed home. |
| **because** | reason | We stayed home **because** it was raining. |

## so vs because
They show the same story from different sides:
= I was tired, **so** I went to bed. (result)
= I went to bed **because** I was tired. (reason)

## Commas
- Often a comma before **but** and **so**: It was late, **so** I took a taxi.
- No comma before **because** in most sentences.

## Lists: a, b **and** c
= I bought rice, dal **and** vegetables.

## Don't use two linking words together
❌ ~~Because I was tired, so I slept.~~ → ✅ **Because** I was tired, I slept. / I was tired, **so** I slept.
[examples]
I called her, but she didn't answer.
Do you want to walk or take the bus?
It was very hot, so we went swimming.
She's happy because she passed her exam.
My brother and I love cricket.
The phone is expensive, but it's very good.
I missed the bus, so I was late.
I didn't go out because I had a cold.
[mistakes]
Because it was late, so I went home. => Because it was late, I went home. | It was late, so I went home. :: Use one linking word.
I like apples but oranges. => I like apples and oranges. :: Adding → and.
I was hungry, because I ate. => I was hungry, so I ate. :: Result → so.
[easy]
mc: I like football ___ tennis. | but | *and | so :: add → and
mc: It's small ___ comfortable. | and so | *but | or :: contrast → but
mc: Would you like tea ___ coffee? | and | *or | so :: choice → or
fill: It was cold, ___ I wore a jacket. = so :: result
fill: I'm happy ___ it's my birthday. = because :: reason
order: I was tired, so I went to bed. :: result → so
[medium]
fix: Because I was hungry, so I ate a sandwich. => Because I was hungry, I ate a sandwich. | I was hungry, so I ate a sandwich. :: one linking word
mc: I studied hard, ___ I failed the exam. | so | *but | because :: surprise → but
fill: She stayed at home ___ she was ill. = because :: reason
mc: We can go today ___ tomorrow. | but | *or | so :: choice → or
fill: The shop was closed, ___ we went home. = so :: result
order: He's rich but he isn't happy. :: contrast
[hard]
mc: "It rained, so the match was cancelled." Which has the same meaning? | *The match was cancelled because it rained. | The match was cancelled, but it rained. | It rained because the match was cancelled. :: so ↔ because
fill: I bought apples, bananas ___ mangoes. = and :: lists → and
fix: I don't like coffee, so I like tea. => I don't like coffee, but I like tea. :: contrast → but
mc: She didn't come to work ___ she was sick. | so | *because | but :: reason
fill: I wanted to go out, ___ it was raining, ___ I stayed home. = but, so :: contrast + result
fix: He's tired because he worked all night, so he is sleeping. => He's sleeping because he worked all night. | He worked all night, so he's sleeping. :: one clear link

# 98 | When… | Conjunctions and clauses | B1
summary: when + clause joins two sentences about time. For the future, use the present after when: When I get home, I'll call you (not when I will get).
[learn]
## when + clause
= **When** I was a child, I lived in a village.
= I was very happy **when** I heard the news.
The *when* part can come **first** (+ comma) or **second** (no comma).

## Future: present tense after *when*
After **when, before, after, while, until, as soon as**, use the **present** for the future:
= **When** I **get** home, I'll call you. (not ~~when I will get~~)
= I'll wait here **until** you **come** back.
= **Before** you **go**, close the windows.
= I'll tell her **as soon as** I **see** her.

## Other time words
| Word | Example |
|---|---|
| **before** | Wash your hands **before** you eat. |
| **after** | **After** we had lunch, we went for a walk. |
| **while** | **While** I was cooking, he set the table. |
| **until** | Wait **until** the light turns green. |
| **as soon as** | Call me **as soon as** you arrive. |
[examples]
When I saw her, she was smiling.
I'll phone you when I arrive in Delhi.
When it rains, the roads flood.
Before you leave, please turn off the lights.
We'll start as soon as everybody is here.
After I finish work, I'll go to the gym.
Don't cross the road until the light is green.
When you're ready, let's go.
[mistakes]
When I will finish, I'll call you. => When I finish, I'll call you. :: Present after when (future).
I'll wait until you will come. => I'll wait until you come. :: Present after until.
As soon as she will arrive, tell me. => As soon as she arrives, tell me. :: Present after as soon as.
[easy]
mc: When I ___ home, I'll make dinner. | will get | *get | got :: present after when
mc: I'll call you when I ___ to Mumbai. | will go | *go | going :: present for future
fill: ___ I was young, I loved cartoons. = When :: when + past
fill: Wash your hands ___ you eat. = before :: before
mc: I'll wait here ___ you come back. | when will | *until | while will :: until + present
order: When I saw him, I was surprised. :: when-clause first + comma
[medium]
fix: When she will come, we will start. => When she comes, we will start. | When she comes, we'll start. :: present after when
fill: As soon as I ___ (hear) anything, I'll tell you. = hear :: present after as soon as
mc: ___ I was cooking, the phone rang. | Until | *While | Before :: while + long action
fill: Let's have a coffee after we ___ (finish) this. = finish :: present after after
fix: I'll stay until the film will end. => I'll stay until the film ends. :: present after until
order: Call me as soon as you arrive. :: as soon as + present
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | I'll be happy when the exams will finish. | *I'll be happy when the exams finish. | I'm happy when the exams will finish. :: present after when
fill: Before you ___ (go) to bed, ___ (lock) the door. = go, lock :: present + imperative
fix: After I will graduate, I will travel. => After I graduate, I will travel. | After I graduate, I'll travel. :: present after after
fill: When she ___ (be) a student, she ___ (live) in a hostel. = was, lived :: past + past
mc: Don't start ___ I tell you. | when will | *until | as :: until + present
fix: When I will be older, I will buy a big house. => When I am older, I will buy a big house. | When I'm older, I'll buy a big house. :: present after when

# 99 | If we go…, If you see… (first conditional) | Conjunctions and clauses | B1
summary: Use if + present, will + base verb for real possible situations in the future: If it rains, we'll stay home.
[learn]
## Form: if + present simple, … will + base verb
= **If** it **rains**, we**'ll stay** at home.
= **If** you **study**, you**'ll pass**.
= I**'ll call** you **if** I **have** time.

The *if* part can be first (+ comma) or second (no comma).

## No *will* after *if*
= ❌ ~~If it will rain…~~ ✅ **If it rains…**

## Other forms in the result part
= **If** you're tired, **go** to bed. (imperative)
= **If** you see Ravi, **can** you give him this? (modal)
= **If** you mix red and white, you **get** pink. (always true → present)

## if vs when
| | |
|---|---|
| **If** I see her, I'll tell her. | maybe I'll see her |
| **When** I see her, I'll tell her. | I'm sure I'll see her |

## unless = if not
= I won't go **unless** you come. (= if you don't come)
[examples]
If you hurry, you'll catch the train.
If it's sunny tomorrow, we'll go to the beach.
What will you do if you don't get the job?
I'll help you if you want.
If you heat ice, it melts. :: always true
If you see Mina, say hello from me.
We'll be late unless we leave now.
If I have time, I'll call you tonight.
[mistakes]
If it will rain, we'll stay home. => If it rains, we'll stay home. :: No will after if.
If you will study, you will pass. => If you study, you will pass. | If you study, you'll pass. :: Present after if.
If I see him, I tell him. (future) => If I see him, I'll tell him. :: Result → will.
[easy]
mc: If it ___ tomorrow, we'll stay home. | will rain | *rains | rained :: if + present
mc: If you study, you ___ the exam. | pass | *will pass | passed :: result → will
fill: If I ___ (see) him, I'll tell him. = see :: if + present
fill: We'll go to the park if it ___ (be) sunny. = is :: if + present
mc: If you're hungry, ___ a sandwich. | you eat | *have | will having :: imperative
order: If you hurry, you will catch the bus. :: if + present, will + base
[medium]
fix: If she will call, I'll answer. => If she calls, I'll answer. | If she calls, I will answer. :: present after if
fill: What ___ you ___ (do) if you miss the flight? = will, do :: will in the result
mc: If you heat water to 100°C, it ___. | will boils | *boils | boiled :: always true → present
fill: I won't go ___ you come with me. (= if you don't) = unless :: unless = if not
fix: If I will have time, I'll visit you. => If I have time, I'll visit you. :: no will after if
order: I'll call you if I need help. :: result first, no comma
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | If it'll snow, the school will close. | *If it snows, the school will close. | If it snows, the school closes tomorrow. :: if + present, will
fill: If you ___ (not / leave) now, you ___ (miss) the train. = don't leave, will miss | don't leave, 'll miss | do not leave, will miss :: negative if + will
fix: Unless you don't hurry, you'll be late. => Unless you hurry, you'll be late. | If you don't hurry, you'll be late. :: unless = if not (no double negative)
mc: "I'm sure I'll see her tonight." → "___ I see her, I'll give her the book." | If | *When | Unless :: sure → when
fill: If you ___ (mix) blue and yellow, you ___ (get) green. = mix, get :: always true → present + present
fix: You'll feel better if you will rest. => You'll feel better if you rest. :: present after if

# 100 | If I had…, If we went… (second conditional) | Conjunctions and clauses | B1
summary: Use if + past, would + base verb for imaginary or unlikely situations now or in the future: If I had a car, I would drive to work.
[learn]
## Form: if + past simple, … would + base verb
= **If** I **had** a car, I **would drive** to work. (but I don't have a car)
= **If** I **won** the lottery, I**'d travel** the world. (unlikely)
= What **would** you **do** if you **lost** your job?

The past here doesn't mean past time — it means **imaginary**.

## were for be (all subjects)
= **If** I **were** you, I**'d** take the job. (advice)
= **If** she **were** here, she'd help us.
(*was* is common in speech, but *were* is better, especially in "If I were you".)

## No *would* after *if*
= ❌ ~~If I would have money…~~ ✅ **If I had money…**

## could / might instead of would
= If I had more time, I **could** learn the piano.
= If you asked him, he **might** help.

## First vs second conditional
| Real / possible | Imaginary / unlikely |
|---|---|
| **If** I **have** time, I**'ll** help. | **If** I **had** time, I**'d** help. (but I don't) |
| **If** it **rains**, we'll stay in. | **If** it **snowed** in Chennai, I'd be amazed! |
[examples]
If I had more money, I would buy a house.
If I were you, I'd see a doctor.
What would you do if you saw a tiger?
If she spoke English, she could get a better job.
I'd go out if it weren't so cold.
If we lived near the sea, we'd go swimming every day.
If I knew his number, I'd call him.
Would you be angry if I told you the truth?
[mistakes]
If I would have a car, I would drive. => If I had a car, I would drive. | If I had a car, I'd drive. :: Past after if.
If I am you, I'll go. => If I were you, I'd go. | If I were you, I would go. :: Advice → If I were you…
If I had wings, I will fly. => If I had wings, I would fly. | If I had wings, I'd fly. :: Result → would.
[easy]
mc: If I ___ rich, I'd buy a big house. | am | *were | will be :: imaginary → were
mc: If I had time, I ___ help you. | will | *would | can :: imaginary → would
fill: If I ___ (have) a dog, I'd walk it every day. = had :: if + past
fill: I'd travel more if I ___ (have) more money. = had :: if + past
mc: If I were you, I ___ that. | won't do | *wouldn't do | don't do :: advice → wouldn't
order: If I won the lottery, I would travel. :: if + past, would + base
[medium]
fix: If I would know the answer, I would tell you. => If I knew the answer, I would tell you. | If I knew the answer, I'd tell you. :: past after if
fill: What ___ you ___ (do) if you found a wallet in the street? = would, do :: would in the result
mc: If she ___ harder, she could pass. | studies | *studied | will study :: imaginary → past
fill: If I ___ (be) taller, I'd play basketball. = were | was :: if + were/was
fix: If I had a car, I will drive you home. => If I had a car, I would drive you home. | If I had a car, I'd drive you home. :: would
order: If I were you, I would apologise. :: advice
[hard]
mc: Which is imaginary? | If it rains, we'll stay in. | *If I lived on the moon, I'd be very light. | When I get home, I'll eat. :: if + past, would
fill: If we ___ (not / have) a test tomorrow, I ___ (go) to the party. = didn't have, would go | didn't have, 'd go | did not have, would go :: imaginary present
fix: If I was you, I'll accept the offer. => If I were you, I'd accept the offer. | If I were you, I would accept the offer. :: If I were you + would
mc: "I don't have his number, so I can't call him." → If I ___ his number, I ___ call him. | have / will | *had / could | had / can :: imaginary → past + could
fill: Would you help me if I ___ (ask) you? = asked :: if + past
fix: I would be happy if you will come. => I would be happy if you came. | I'd be happy if you came. :: past after if

# 101 | a person who…, a thing that/which… (relative clauses 1) | Conjunctions and clauses | B1
summary: Relative clauses tell us which person or thing we mean. Use who for people and which/that for things: the man who lives next door, the bus that goes to the airport.
[learn]
## who for people, which / that for things
= The woman **who** lives next door is a nurse.
= I have a friend **who** speaks five languages.
= The bus **which/that** goes to the airport is number 5.
= Where's the book **that** was on the table?

*that* can also be used for people (informal): the man **that** helped me.

## Why use them?
To join two sentences and say **which one** we mean:
= A man helped me. He was very kind. → The man **who** helped me was very kind.
= I lost a ring. It was my mother's. → I lost a ring **that** was my mother's.

## Here *who/which/that* is the SUBJECT of the clause
It is followed by a verb:
= the girl **who won** the prize
= the train **that leaves** at 8

## Don't repeat the subject
= ❌ ~~The man who he called me~~ ✅ The man **who called** me
= ❌ ~~The phone which it broke~~ ✅ The phone **which broke**
[examples]
A dentist is a person who looks after your teeth.
The shop that sells phones is closed today.
I met a woman who works at the bank.
This is the key which opens the front door.
Do you know the boy who is standing over there?
The film that won the award is excellent.
People who exercise regularly live longer.
I've lost the bag that I bought yesterday.
[mistakes]
The man which lives here is a doctor. => The man who lives here is a doctor. :: People → who.
The car who is outside is mine. => The car which is outside is mine. | The car that is outside is mine. :: Things → which/that.
The girl who she sings is my sister. => The girl who sings is my sister. :: Don't repeat the subject.
[easy]
mc: A teacher is a person ___ teaches. | which | *who | where :: people → who
mc: This is the bus ___ goes to the station. | who | *that | what :: things → that/which
fill: I know a man ___ can speak Japanese. = who | that :: people → who
fill: The cake ___ is on the table is for you. = which | that :: things
mc: The woman ___ lives upstairs is very friendly. | which | *who | whose :: people
order: The film that we saw was great. :: relative clause after the noun
[medium]
fix: The book who I'm reading is great. => The book that I'm reading is great. | The book which I'm reading is great. | The book I'm reading is great. :: things → that/which
mc: A thief is someone ___ steals things. | which | *who | what :: people → who
fill: The road ___ goes to the beach is closed. = that | which :: things
fix: The boy who he won the race is my cousin. => The boy who won the race is my cousin. :: don't repeat the subject
mc: Where are the keys ___ were on the table? | who | *that | what :: things
order: I have a friend who lives in Paris. :: who + verb
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | The man what helped me was kind. | *The man who helped me was kind. | The man which helped me was kind. :: people → who
fill: "A dictionary is a book ___ gives the meanings of words." = that | which :: things
fix: People which smoke damage their health. => People who smoke damage their health. :: people → who
fill: I spoke to the doctor ___ treated my father. = who | that :: people
mc: The phone ___ I bought last week has stopped working. | who | what | *that :: things
fix: The house which it is on the corner is for sale. => The house which is on the corner is for sale. | The house that is on the corner is for sale. :: don't repeat the subject

# 102 | the people we met, the hotel you stayed at (relative clauses 2) | Conjunctions and clauses | B1
summary: When who/which/that is the object of the clause, you can leave it out: the film (that) we watched, the people (who) we met. Prepositions go at the end: the hotel we stayed at.
[learn]
## Object relative clauses — you can leave out who/which/that
When there is a new subject after *who/which/that*, it's the **object**, so you can drop it:
= The film **(that)** we watched was boring.
= The people **(who)** we met were friendly.
= Is this the book **(which)** you wanted?

| Subject relative (must keep) | Object relative (can drop) |
|---|---|
| The man **who called** me… | The man **(who) I called**… |
| The bus **that goes** to town… | The bus **(that) we took**… |

## Prepositions at the end
= The hotel **we stayed at** was lovely. (stay at a hotel)
= Who's the girl **you were talking to**?
= This is the music **I was listening to**.
= The chair **he was sitting on** broke.

## where
For places, you can use **where**: the town **where** I was born (= the town I was born in).

## whose = possession
= That's the man **whose** car was stolen.
= I have a friend **whose** father is a pilot.
[examples]
The cake you made was delicious.
Did you find the keys you lost?
The people I work with are very nice.
That's the house where my grandparents lived.
The hotel we stayed at had a pool.
I've got a friend whose sister is an actress.
The song you were singing is my favourite.
Is this the address you were looking for?
[mistakes]
The film we watched it was long. => The film we watched was long. :: Don't repeat the object.
The hotel we stayed was nice. => The hotel we stayed at was nice. | The hotel where we stayed was nice. :: Keep the preposition (stay at).
The man who his car was stolen called the police. => The man whose car was stolen called the police. :: Possession → whose.
[easy]
mc: The pizza ___ we ordered was cold. | who | *that | what :: things → that (you can also leave it out)
mc: The music I was listening ___ was relaxing. | — | *to | at :: listen to → preposition at the end
fill: Is this the shirt ___ you bought? = that | which :: object relative (can also be left out)
mc: That's the town ___ I was born. | which | *where | who :: places → where
fill: I have a friend ___ mother is a doctor. = whose :: possession
order: The book you gave me is great. :: object relative, no "that"
[medium]
fix: The man I met him was a writer. => The man I met was a writer. :: don't repeat the object
fill: Who is the boy you were talking ___? = to :: talk to
mc: Which sentence can drop "that"? | The bus that goes to town. | *The bus that we took. | The bus that is late. :: object → can drop
fill: The restaurant ___ we had dinner was very expensive. = where :: place
fix: The woman who her son is ill is very worried. => The woman whose son is ill is very worried. :: whose
order: The film we watched last night was funny. :: noun + subject + verb
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | The hotel we stayed was cheap. | *The hotel we stayed at was cheap. | The hotel we stayed it was cheap. :: stay at
fill: The chair you're sitting ___ is broken. = on :: sit on
fix: The people which we met were from Brazil. => The people we met were from Brazil. | The people who we met were from Brazil. | The people that we met were from Brazil. :: people → who/that, or nothing
mc: "This is the town where I grew up." = This is the town I grew up ___. | to | *in | at :: grow up in
fill: I met a man ___ dog had learnt to open doors! = whose :: possession
fix: Did you like the presents what I gave you? => Did you like the presents I gave you? | Did you like the presents that I gave you? :: what → that / nothing

# 103 | at 8 o'clock, on Monday, in April (prepositions of time) | Prepositions | A1
summary: at + clock times (at 6), on + days and dates (on Monday, on 5th May), in + months, years, seasons and parts of the day (in July, in the morning).
[learn]
## at, on, in
| at | on | in |
|---|---|---|
| times: **at** 7 o'clock, **at** noon | days: **on** Monday | months: **in** March |
| **at** night | dates: **on** 15th August | years: **in** 2024 |
| **at** the weekend (UK) | **on** my birthday | seasons: **in** summer |
| **at** Christmas / Diwali (the holiday period) | **on** Christmas Day | **in** the morning / afternoon / evening |
| **at** the moment / the same time | **on** Friday morning | decades / centuries: **in** the 1990s |

= I get up **at** 6.
= We have a meeting **on** Tuesday.
= She was born **in** 1998.
= I study best **in** the morning but sleep **at** night.

## No preposition with this / last / next / every / today / tomorrow
= I'll see you **next** week. (not ~~in next week~~)
= We met **last** Monday. (not ~~on last Monday~~)
= I go swimming **every** day.

## in + period = after that time from now
= The train leaves **in** five minutes.
= I'll be back **in** an hour.
[examples]
The film starts at 8.30.
I was born on 3rd March.
We usually go on holiday in May.
My birthday is in winter.
I work late on Fridays.
He'll call you back in ten minutes.
I don't like walking alone at night.
See you next week!
[mistakes]
I'll see you on next Friday. => I'll see you next Friday. :: No preposition with next.
The class starts on 9 o'clock. => The class starts at 9 o'clock. :: Times → at.
I was born at 2001. => I was born in 2001. :: Years → in.
[easy]
mc: The bus leaves ___ 7 o'clock. | on | *at | in :: clock times → at
mc: I was born ___ July. | on | at | *in :: months → in
mc: We play cricket ___ Saturday. | in | at | *on :: days → on
fill: She always reads ___ night. = at :: at night
fill: It's cold ___ winter. = in :: seasons → in
order: My birthday is on 12th June. :: dates → on
[medium]
fix: I'll call you on next week. => I'll call you next week. :: no preposition with next
mc: I'll be ready ___ five minutes. | after | *in | on :: in + period = from now
fill: We met ___ my birthday. = on :: on my birthday
mc: I usually go running ___ the morning. | at | on | *in :: in the morning
fill: The shops are closed ___ Christmas Day. = on :: specific day → on
order: Let's meet at noon tomorrow. :: at noon
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | in Monday morning | *on Monday morning | at Monday morning :: day + part of day → on
fill: The Taj Mahal was built ___ the 17th century. = in :: centuries → in
fix: We go to the beach at every Sunday. => We go to the beach every Sunday. :: no preposition with every
fill: Shops are busy ___ Diwali. (the festival period) = at | during :: holiday period → at
mc: I'm busy ___ the moment. | in | *at | on :: at the moment
fix: She was born in 25th December. => She was born on 25th December. | She was born on the 25th of December. :: dates → on

# 104 | from…to, until, since, for | Prepositions | A2
summary: from…to/until = the start and end. until = up to a time. since = from a point in the past until now. for = a period of time.
[learn]
## from … to / from … until
= The bank is open **from** 9 **to** 5. / **from** 9 **until** 5.
= I lived in Delhi **from** 2015 **to** 2020.

## until (till) = up to a time, then it stops
= I'll wait **until** 6 o'clock.
= We stayed **until** the end of the film.
= The shop is open **until** midnight.

## since = from a point in the past until NOW (present perfect)
= I've lived here **since** 2018.
= She's been ill **since** Monday.

## for = a period of time (any tense)
= I stayed in Goa **for** two weeks.
= I've worked here **for** five years.
= We're going away **for** a few days.

## Summary
| | Example |
|---|---|
| from … to | from Monday to Friday |
| until | until 10 pm (then stop) |
| since + start point | since 9 am / since 2010 (until now) |
| for + period | for an hour / for three years |

> **by** = not later than: Please finish **by** Friday. (≠ until: work **until** Friday = keep working then stop)
[examples]
I work from Monday to Friday.
The museum is open until 6 pm.
We've been friends since childhood.
She lived in London for ten years.
I'll be at home until 4. Then I'm going out.
It's been raining since this morning.
Please send the report by Thursday.
The concert went on until midnight.
[mistakes]
I've lived here since five years. => I've lived here for five years. :: Period → for.
I'll wait you until you come. => I'll wait for you until you come. :: wait for someone.
Please reply until Monday. (not later than) => Please reply by Monday. :: deadline → by.
[easy]
mc: The shop is open ___ 9 to 5. | since | *from | for :: from … to

| look **for** | I'm **looking for** my keys. (searching) |
| wait **for** | We're **waiting for** the bus. |
| ask **for** | He **asked for** the bill. |
| talk / speak **to** (or **with**) | Can I **talk to** you? |
| talk **about** | What are you **talking about**? |
| think **about / of** | I'm **thinking about** you. |
| depend **on** | It **depends on** the weather. |
| belong **to** | This bag **belongs to** Ana. |
| pay **for** | I'll **pay for** the tickets. |
| apply **for** | She **applied for** a job. |
| happen **to** | What **happened to** your car? |
| laugh **at** | Everyone **laughed at** the joke. |

## No preposition!
= **call / phone** someone (not ~~call to~~)
= **discuss** something (not ~~discuss about~~)
= **enter** a room · **marry** someone · **answer** a question · **reach** a place

## In questions, the preposition stays at the end
= Who are you **waiting for**?
= What are you **looking at**?
= It **depends**. (no object → no *on*)
[examples]
Listen to me carefully.
What are you looking at?
I'm looking for a cheap hotel.
We waited for an hour.
This phone belongs to my brother.
Can I pay for this by card?
Let's discuss the plan tomorrow.
I'll call you tonight.
[mistakes]
I'm listening music. => I'm listening to music. :: listen to
We discussed about the problem. => We discussed the problem. :: discuss (no about)
I'll call to you later. => I'll call you later. :: call someone (no to)
[easy]
mc: I like listening ___ the radio. | at | *to | for :: listen to
mc: Look ___ that beautiful bird! | to | *at | for :: look at
mc: We're waiting ___ the train. | to | *for | at :: wait for
fill: This book belongs ___ my teacher. = to :: belong to
fill: I'm looking ___ my glasses. Have you seen them? = for :: look for = search
order: Who are you talking to? :: preposition at the end
[medium]
fix: I'll phone to you tomorrow. => I'll phone you tomorrow. :: phone someone
mc: "Are you coming?" "It depends ___ the weather." | of | *on | to :: depend on
fill: How much did you pay ___ that jacket? = for :: pay for
fix: We need to discuss about your holiday. => We need to discuss your holiday. :: discuss (no about)
mc: She applied ___ a job at the bank. | to | *for | on :: apply for
order: What are you looking at? :: look at + question
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | He entered into the room. | *He entered the room. | He entered to the room. :: enter (no preposition)
fill: What happened ___ your arm? Is it broken? = to :: happen to
fix: Don't laugh to me! => Don't laugh at me! :: laugh at

# 114 | go in, fall off, run away (phrasal verbs 1) | Phrasal verbs | B1
summary: Phrasal verbs = verb + a small word (in, out, on, off, up, down, away, back, over, round). The meaning is often movement: come in, sit down, go away, get up, look round.
[learn]
## Verb + particle
| Particle | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **in / out** | enter / leave | come **in**, go **out**, get **out** |
| **on / off** | onto / away from | get **on** the bus, fall **off**, get **off** |
| **up / down** | higher / lower | stand **up**, sit **down**, get **up**, lie **down** |
| **away** | to another place | go **away**, run **away**, walk **away** |
| **back** | return | come **back**, go **back**, call **back** |
| **over / round** | to the other side / turn | fall **over**, turn **round**, look **round** |
| **along / about / around** | moving | walk **around**, come **along** |

= Please **come in** and **sit down**.
= I **got up** at 6.
= The thief **ran away**.
= When are you **coming back**?
= Be careful! Don't **fall off** the wall.

## Other meanings (not movement)
= The plane **took off**. (left the ground)
= **Look out!** (be careful)
= The car **broke down**. (stopped working)
= **Hurry up!**
= Please **go on**. (continue)
= She **gets on** well with her boss. (has a good relationship)
= Our holiday is **over**. (finished)

> Phrasal verbs are very common in spoken English. Learn them as one unit of meaning.
[examples]
Come in! The door's open.
What time do you usually get up?
The children ran out into the garden.
Go away! I'm busy.
I'll call you back in five minutes.
He fell over and hurt his knee.
Our car broke down on the motorway.
The plane took off on time.
[mistakes]
Please sit. (asking someone to take a seat) => Please sit down. :: sit down
I woke at 6 and got out the bed. => I woke up at 6 and got out of bed. :: get out of bed
I will return back tomorrow. => I will come back tomorrow. | I will return tomorrow. :: come back = return (don't say "return back")
[easy]
mc: Please come ___ and take a seat. | out | *in | off :: enter → come in
mc: I get ___ at 7 every morning. | down | *up | on :: get up
mc: Be careful! Don't fall ___ your bike. | out | *off | in :: fall off
fill: The dog ran ___ and never came back. = away :: run away
fill: When will you come ___ from your trip? = back :: come back
order: Please sit down and relax. :: sit down
[medium]
fix: Hurry! The plane is taking out. => Hurry! The plane is taking off. :: take off
mc: Our car broke ___ on the highway. | up | *down | off :: break down = stop working
fill: Look ___! There's a car coming! = out :: look out = be careful
mc: I'm busy. Can I call you ___ later? | again | *back | over :: call back
fill: Don't stop. Please go ___ with your story. = on :: go on = continue
order: She turned round and smiled at me. :: turn round
[hard]
mc: "I get on well with my sister." means: | I travel with her. | *We have a good relationship. | I get on a bus with her. :: get on with = good relationship
fix: The film is finished over. => The film is over. :: be over = finished
fill: He tripped and fell ___ in the street. = over | down :: fall over = fall to the ground
mc: Let's look ___ the old town. | after | *round | out :: look round = visit, see the place
fill: Hurry ___! We're going to miss the bus. = up :: hurry up
fix: The children got up the bus at the school. => The children got off the bus at the school. :: get off a bus

# 115 | put on your shoes / put your shoes on (phrasal verbs 2) | Phrasal verbs | B1
summary: Some phrasal verbs take an object: put on, take off, turn on/off, pick up, throw away, fill in. With a noun, the object can go before or after the particle; with it/them, it must go in the middle: put it on.
[learn]
## Two positions for a noun object
| | |
|---|---|
| **put on** your shoes | **put** your shoes **on** |
| **turn off** the light | **turn** the light **off** |
| **pick up** the phone | **pick** the phone **up** |

Both are correct.

## Pronouns (it / them / me…) → in the middle ONLY
= ✅ **Put it on**. ❌ ~~Put on it.~~
= ✅ **Turn them off**. ❌ ~~Turn off them.~~
= ✅ Can you **pick me up** at 6?

## Common phrasal verbs with objects
| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| put on / take off | clothes on / off | **Take off** your shoes. |
| turn on / turn off | switch on / off | **Turn** the TV **on**. |
| turn up / turn down | louder / quieter | **Turn** the music **down**. |
| pick up | lift; collect someone | **Pick** your toys **up**. I'll **pick** you **up**. |
| throw away | put in the rubbish | Don't **throw** it **away**! |
| fill in | complete a form | **Fill in** this form. |
| try on | test clothes | Can I **try** these jeans **on**? |
| give back | return | **Give** my pen **back**! |
| write down | make a note | **Write** it **down**. |
| look up | find in a dictionary | **Look** the word **up**. |
| wake up | stop someone sleeping | Don't **wake** the baby **up**. |
| give up | stop a habit | He **gave up** smoking. |
[examples]
It's cold. Put on your jacket.
Take your shoes off before you come in.
Can you turn the light off, please?
I'll pick you up from the airport.
Don't throw those letters away.
Please fill in this form.
Can I try these shoes on?
If you don't know the word, look it up.
[mistakes]
Turn off it. => Turn it off. :: Pronoun → in the middle.
Can I try on them? => Can I try them on? :: Pronoun → in the middle.
Please write down it. => Please write it down. :: Pronoun → in the middle.
[easy]
mc: It's cold. Put your coat ___. | in | *on | up :: put on clothes
mc: Please turn ___ the TV. I'm sleeping. | on | *off | out :: turn off
mc: Here is your hat. Put ___. | on it | *it on | it in :: pronoun in the middle
fill: Take ___ your shoes, please. = off :: take off
fill: Don't throw it ___! I still need it. = away :: throw away
order: Can you pick me up at six? :: pronoun in the middle
[medium]
fix: The music is too loud. Turn down it. => The music is too loud. Turn it down. :: pronoun in the middle
mc: I don't know this word. I'll look ___. | up it | *it up | it after :: look it up
fill: Please fill ___ this form. = in :: fill in
mc: Can I try ___? (these jeans) | on them | *them on | them in :: pronoun in the middle
fill: My brother gave ___ smoking last year. = up :: give up = stop
order: Write your phone number down. :: noun can go in the middle
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | Turn on them. | *Turn them on. | Turn on it them. :: pronoun in the middle
fix: Don't wake up her. She's tired. => Don't wake her up. She's tired. :: pronoun in the middle
fill: That's my book! Give it ___ to me! = back :: give back
mc: "The phone is ringing." "Can you pick ___?" | up it | *it up | up :: pronoun in the middle
fill: I'll put the dishes away and you take the rubbish ___. = out :: take out the rubbish
fix: Put off the light when you leave. => Turn off the light when you leave. | Turn the light off when you leave. | Switch off the light when you leave. :: turn off a light
