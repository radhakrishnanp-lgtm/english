# 65 | a / an | A and the | A1
summary: Use a/an with one countable thing (a book, an apple). Use a before a consonant sound and an before a vowel sound. Use a/an for jobs.
[learn]
## a or an? It depends on the SOUND
| a + consonant sound | an + vowel sound |
|---|---|
| **a** book, **a** car | **an** apple, **an** egg |
| **a** university (/juː/) | **an** hour (h is silent) |
| **a** European country (/jʊ/) | **an** honest man |
| **a** one-way ticket (/w/) | **an** MP3 player (/em/) |

## Use a/an for…
- **one** thing (singular countable): I have **a** brother. She ate **an** orange.
- saying **what something is**: It's **a** temple. That's **an** elephant.
- **jobs**: She's **a** doctor. He's **an** engineer. (not ~~She's doctor~~)
- **what something is like**: It's **a** big city. He's **a** nice man.

## Don't use a/an with…
- plurals: ~~a books~~ → books / some books
- uncountable nouns: ~~a water~~ / ~~a advice~~ → water / some advice
- possessives: ~~a my friend~~ → my friend / a friend of mine

## a/an = one, per
= twice **a** week, 60 km **an** hour, 100 rupees **a** kilo
[examples]
I bought a new umbrella yesterday.
She is an architect.
We waited for an hour.
He's a university student.
Is there a bank near here?
It's an interesting idea.
I go swimming three times a week.
That's a lovely photo!
[mistakes]
She's teacher. => She's a teacher. | She is a teacher. :: Jobs need a/an.
I ate a apple. => I ate an apple. :: Vowel sound → an.
He has an university degree. => He has a university degree. :: "university" starts with a /j/ sound → a.
[easy]
mc: I have ___ sister. | an | *a | — :: consonant sound → a
mc: She ate ___ egg for breakfast. | a | *an | — :: vowel sound → an
mc: My father is ___ engineer. | a | *an | — :: jobs + vowel sound → an
fill: It takes ___ hour to get there. = an :: silent h → an
fill: Mumbai is ___ big city. = a :: a + adjective + noun
order: My brother is an actor. :: job → a/an
[medium]
fix: He wants to be pilot. => He wants to be a pilot. :: jobs need a/an
mc: It's ___ unusual name. | a | *an | — :: vowel sound → an
mc: She goes to ___ university in Delhi. | an | *a | — :: /juː/ sound → a
fill: I drink coffee twice ___ day. = a :: a = per
fix: I need an information. => I need some information. | I need information. :: uncountable → no a/an
order: Is there a pharmacy near here? :: a + singular noun
[hard]
mc: He's ___ honest person. | a | *an | — :: silent h → an
mc: It's ___ European country. | an | *a | — :: "European" starts with a /j/ sound → a
fill: The speed limit is 80 km ___ hour. = an :: an hour (per)
fix: They are a good friends. => They are good friends. :: plural → no a
mc: I bought ___ one-way ticket. | an | *a | — :: "one" starts with /w/ → a

# 66 | train(s), bus(es) (singular and plural) | A and the | A1
summary: Make plurals with -s (cars), -es (buses), -ies (cities), -ves (knives) and learn irregular plurals (children, people, men, feet, teeth).
[learn]
## Regular plurals
| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| most nouns + **-s** | car → car**s**, book → book**s** |
| -s, -sh, -ch, -x + **-es** | bus → bus**es**, dish → dish**es**, watch → watch**es**, box → box**es** |
| consonant + y → **-ies** | city → cit**ies**, baby → bab**ies** |
| vowel + y → **-s** | day → day**s**, key → key**s** |
| -f / -fe → **-ves** | knife → kni**ves**, leaf → lea**ves**, wife → wi**ves** |
| -o → -es (some) | potato → potato**es**, tomato → tomato**es** (but photo**s**, radio**s**) |

## Irregular plurals
| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| man / woman | **men / women** |
| child | **children** |
| person | **people** |
| foot / tooth | **feet / teeth** |
| mouse | **mice** |
| sheep / fish | **sheep / fish** (no change) |

## Always plural
**trousers, jeans, shorts, glasses (for eyes), scissors, pyjamas** take a plural verb:
= My jeans **are** too long. (not ~~is~~)
= Where **are** my glasses?
= a **pair of** trousers / scissors

> **people** is plural: People **are** friendly here. *police* is plural too: The police **are** coming.
[examples]

I need three boxes for my books.
Many cities in India are very old.
The children are playing in the park.
My feet hurt after the long walk.
These scissors are very sharp.
How many people live in your house?
Cut the tomatoes into small pieces.
[mistakes]
I have two childs. => I have two children. :: child → children
These peoples are friendly. => These people are friendly. :: person → people
My trousers is dirty. => My trousers are dirty. :: trousers is always plural

[medium]

fill: one city, many ___ = cities :: consonant + y → ies

[hard]

summary: Use both, either and neither for two things or people: both (= the two), either (= one or the other), neither (= not one and not the other).

## Only for TWO
| | Meaning | Example |

| **both** | the two | **Both** restaurants are good. |
| **either** | one or the other (it doesn't matter) | You can sit in **either** seat. |

## + noun
- **both** + plural noun: **both** sisters, **both** cars

# 83 | a lot, much, many | Determiners and pronouns | A2
summary: Use many with plural countable nouns and much with uncountable nouns, mostly in questions and negatives. Use a lot (of) in positive sentences.
[learn]
## many / much
| many + plural | much + uncountable |
|---|---|
| **many** books, **many** people | **much** money, **much** time |
| How **many** brothers have you got? | How **much** water do you drink? |
| I don't have **many** friends here. | I don't have **much** time. |

## a lot of / lots of (+ plural or uncountable)
Most common in **positive** sentences:
= I have **a lot of** friends.
= She drinks **lots of** coffee.
= There was **a lot of** traffic.

## In positive sentences
*much* sounds very formal in positive sentences. Prefer *a lot of*:
= ✅ I have **a lot of** money. (❌ ~~I have much money~~ — unusual)
*many* is OK in positive sentences but *a lot of* is more common.

## Without a noun: a lot (no *of*)
= She talks **a lot**.
= "Do you watch TV?" "Not **much**."
= I like it **a lot**.

## too much / too many / so much / so many
= There are **too many** cars in this city.
= Thank you **so much**!
[examples]
How many people came to the meeting?
We don't have much time. Hurry up!
There are a lot of tourists in Goa in December.
I don't eat much meat.
She has lots of books.
Do you travel much?
There were too many people on the bus.
I ate a lot at the wedding.
[mistakes]
How much brothers do you have? => How many brothers do you have? :: countable → many.
I don't have many money. => I don't have much money. :: uncountable → much.
She talks a lot of. => She talks a lot. :: no noun → a lot.

# 84 | (a) little, (a) few | Determiners and pronouns | B1
summary: a few + plural (a few friends), a little + uncountable (a little milk) = some. few/little without a = not much/many (negative feeling).
[learn]
## a few / a little = some, a small number/amount (positive)
| a few + plural countable | a little + uncountable |
|---|---|
| **a few** days, **a few** people | **a little** time, **a little** water |
= I've got **a few** friends here. (some — that's good)
= Can I have **a little** milk in my tea?
= We stayed **a few** days in Mysore.

## few / little (without *a*) = not many / not much (negative)
= He's very shy. He has **few** friends. (not many — that's sad)
= There's **little** hope. (not much)
*few/little* alone are formal. In conversation we usually say *not many / not much*.

## Compare
| | |
|---|---|
| I have **a little** money. Let's get a coffee. | (some — enough) |
| I have **little** money. I can't buy lunch. | (not enough) |

## very little / very few / only a few / only a little
= Very **few** people came.
= We have **only a little** time.
[examples]
I need a few minutes to get ready.
Could I have a little more rice, please?
A few of my friends are coming over.
I speak a little Hindi.
Few people know this secret.
There's very little petrol in the car.
We waited a few hours at the airport.
He drinks a little coffee every morning.

I have a little friends. => I have a few friends. :: countable → a few.
Can I have a few water? => Can I have a little water? :: uncountable → a little.
I speak few English. => I speak a little English. :: uncountable → a little.
