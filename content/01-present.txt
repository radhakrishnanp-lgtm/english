# 1 | am / is / are | Present | A1
summary: The verb "be" in the present: I am, you are, she is… and the short forms I'm, you're, she's.
[learn]
## The verb *be* in the present
*Be* is the most important verb in English. In the present it has three forms: **am**, **is** and **are**.

| Subject | Full form | Short form | Negative (short) |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **am** | I'm | I'm not |
| he / she / it | **is** | he's / she's / it's | he isn't / she's not |
| we / you / they | **are** | we're / you're / they're | we aren't / they're not |

= I'm a student.
= My sister is a nurse.
= We are from Chennai.
= It's cold today.

## Negative: add *not*
Put **not** after am / is / are.
= I'm not hungry.
= The shop isn't open. (= is not)
= They aren't at home. (= are not)

> There is no short form "amn't". Say **I'm not**.

## What do we use *be* for?
- Names and jobs: **I'm** Ravi. **She's** a teacher.
- Age: **He's** twelve. (not ~~He has twelve.~~)
- Places: **They're** in the kitchen.
- Feelings and states: **I'm** tired. **We're** late.
- Descriptions: The film **is** long. The tea **is** hot.

> Plural nouns take **are**: My parents **are** in Delhi. The books **are** on the table.
[examples]
I'm twenty-three years old. :: age uses be, not have
My brother is a software engineer.
Your keys are on the table. :: plural noun → are
It's a beautiful day.
We're not ready yet. :: we are not
This coffee isn't very hot.
Anita and I are good friends. :: "Anita and I" = we → are
The window is open.
[mistakes]
I have 25 years. => I am 25 years old. :: For age, use be.
My friends is here. => My friends are here. :: Plural subject → are.
He not is tired. => He isn't tired. | He's not tired. :: Not comes after the verb be.
[easy]
mc: I ___ a student. | *am | is | are :: I → am
mc: She ___ from Kerala. | am | *is | are :: she → is
mc: They ___ my cousins. | am | is | *are :: they → are
fill: It ___ very hot today. = is | 's :: it → is
fill: We ___ in the classroom. = are | 're :: we → are
order: My mother is a doctor. :: subject + be + a/an + job
[medium]
mc: The children ___ in the garden. | is | *are | am :: "the children" is plural → are
fill: I ___ (not) hungry, thanks. = am not | 'm not :: I'm not — there is no "amn't"
fill: The bank ___ (not) open on Sunday. = isn't | is not | 's not :: the bank = it → isn't
mc: My phone and my wallet ___ in my bag. | is | *are | am :: two things → are
fix: My father have 50 years. => My father is 50 years old. | My father is 50. :: age → be
order: They aren't at work today. :: they + aren't + place
[hard]
mc: Sara and I ___ in the same class. | is | am | *are :: Sara and I = we → are
fix: The news are very good today. => The news is very good today. | The news is very good. :: "news" is uncountable → is
fill: My glasses ___ dirty. I can't see! = are | 're :: glasses (for eyes) is plural → are
mc: Which sentence is correct? | *I'm not late. | I amn't late. | I not am late. :: only "I'm not"
fix: She's a engineer. => She's an engineer. | She is an engineer. :: an before a vowel sound
fill: Everybody ___ happy with the result. = is | 's :: everybody takes a singular verb → is

# 2 | am / is / are — questions | Present | A1
summary: Make questions with be by putting am, is or are before the subject. Short answers: Yes, I am. / No, it isn't.
[learn]
## Making questions
In questions, **am / is / are** comes **before** the subject.

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| You **are** tired. | **Are** you tired? |
| She **is** at home. | **Is** she at home? |
| They **are** ready. | **Are** they ready? |

= Am I late?
= Is your brother married?
= Are you from Mumbai?

## Questions with question words
Question word + **am/is/are** + subject:
= Where **is** the station?
= How old **are** you?
= What **is** your name? → What's your name?
= Why **are** you angry?
= Who **is** that man? → Who's that man?

## Short answers
| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| Yes, I **am**. | No, I'**m not**. |
| Yes, he/she/it **is**. | No, he/she/it **isn't**. |
| Yes, we/you/they **are**. | No, we/you/they **aren't**. |

> In positive short answers, do NOT use the short form: say **Yes, I am.** (not ~~Yes, I'm.~~) and **Yes, she is.** (not ~~Yes, she's.~~)

## Useful question phrases
- How **are** you? — I'm fine, thanks.
- What colour **is** it? — It's blue.
- How much **is** this? / How much **are** these?
- What **is** the weather like?
[examples]
Are you hungry? — Yes, I am.
Is it far from here? — No, it isn't.
Where are my glasses? :: plural → are
How old is your daughter? — She's seven.
What's your phone number?
Are your parents at home? — No, they aren't.
Why is the door open?
How much are these shoes?
[mistakes]
You are ready? => Are you ready? :: Put are before the subject.
Yes, I'm. => Yes, I am. :: No short form in positive short answers.
Where your sister is? => Where is your sister? :: Question word + is + subject.
[easy]
mc: ___ you a student? | Is | *Are | Am :: you → are
mc: ___ she your sister? | *Is | Are | Am :: she → is
mc: Are you tired? — Yes, I ___. | 'm | *am | is :: Yes, I am (no short form)
fill: ___ your name Priya? = Is :: your name = it → is
fill: Where ___ the bathroom? = is :: the bathroom → is
order: Are you from Bangalore? :: Are + you + …?
[medium]
mc: Is the museum open today? — No, it ___. | not | *isn't | aren't :: No, it isn't
fill: How old ___ your children? = are :: children → are
fill: ___ I in the right room? = Am :: I → am
mc: ___ these your bags? | Is | *Are | Am :: these → are
fix: Your friend is angry with me? => Is your friend angry with me? :: is + subject in questions
order: Why are you so happy today? :: Why + are + you …?
[hard]
mc: Are you and Tom brothers? — Yes, we ___. | *are | 're | do :: positive short answer → full form "we are"
fix: How much is these apples? => How much are these apples? :: apples (plural) → are
fill: What colour ___ your eyes? = are :: eyes → are
mc: Is your car new? — Yes, ___. | it's | *it is | is it :: positive short answer → it is
fix: Where are the bus station? => Where is the bus station? :: one station → is
order: What is the weather like in London? :: What + is + the weather + like?

# 3 | Present continuous: I am doing | Present | A1
summary: Use am/is/are + -ing for actions happening now or around now: I'm reading. They're working.
[learn]
## Form: am / is / are + verb-ing
| | |
|---|---|
| I | **am** (I'm) work**ing** |
| he / she / it | **is** (he's…) work**ing** |
| we / you / they | **are** (we're…) work**ing** |

= I'm reading a book.
= She's cooking dinner.
= They're playing football.

## Use: something happening **now**
The action has started and is not finished yet.
= Please be quiet. The baby **is sleeping**.
= Look! It**'s raining**.
= "Where's Ali?" "He**'s having** a shower."

## Use: something happening around now (not exactly this second)
= I**'m learning** English this year.
= Maya **is working** in a hotel this summer.

## Negative
= I**'m not watching** TV. You can turn it off.
= He **isn't working** today. He's at home.
= We **aren't going** out tonight.

## Spelling of -ing
| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| most verbs + -ing | work → work**ing**, eat → eat**ing** |
| -e → drop e | make → mak**ing**, write → writ**ing** |
| short verb (consonant-vowel-consonant) → double | sit → si**tt**ing, run → ru**nn**ing, swim → swi**mm**ing |
| -ie → -ying | lie → l**ying**, die → d**ying** |

> We do not double the letter in words like *open → opening* or *visit → visiting* (the stress is not at the end).
[examples]
I'm waiting for the bus. :: now
Be careful! The floor is getting wet.
My parents are watching the news.
She isn't listening to me. :: negative
We're staying with friends this week. :: around now
The children are swimming in the lake. :: swim → swimming
Hurry up! Everybody is waiting.
I'm not wearing a coat because it's warm.
[mistakes]
She cooking now. => She is cooking now. | She's cooking now. :: Don't forget am/is/are.
He is siting on the floor. => He is sitting on the floor. :: sit → sitting (double t)
They are writeing letters. => They are writing letters. :: write → writing (drop e)
[easy]
mc: Listen! Someone ___ the piano. | *is playing | are playing | playing :: someone → is + -ing
mc: I ___ to music right now. | am listen | *am listening | listening :: am + -ing
fill: We ___ (wait) for a taxi. = are waiting | 're waiting :: we are + waiting
fill: Look! The cat ___ (sleep) on my bed. = is sleeping | 's sleeping :: is + sleeping
mc: They ___ dinner at the moment. | is having | *are having | having :: they → are
order: She is reading a newspaper. :: subject + is + -ing + object
[medium]
fill: The kids ___ (swim) in the pool. = are swimming | 're swimming :: swim → swimming
fill: Shh! The baby ___ (not / sleep) well. = isn't sleeping | is not sleeping | 's not sleeping :: isn't + -ing
mc: Turn off the TV. I ___ it. | am watching | *'m not watching | not watching :: negative: 'm not + -ing
fill: Why ___ you ___ (smile)? = are, smiling :: Why + are + you + -ing
fix: I am make a cake. => I am making a cake. | I'm making a cake. :: am + making
order: It isn't raining any more. :: isn't + raining
[hard]
mc: Which spelling is correct? | runing | *running | runnning :: run → running (double n)
fill: Raj ___ (study) medicine at university this year. = is studying | 's studying :: around now
fix: Look! The bus come. => Look! The bus is coming. | Look! The bus's coming. :: now → is coming
mc: Tom ___ in a café this summer. | works now | *is working | working :: temporary period → present continuous
fix: We are lieing on the beach. => We are lying on the beach. | We're lying on the beach. :: lie → lying
fill: I ___ (not / feel) very well today. = am not feeling | 'm not feeling :: I'm not + feeling

# 4 | Present continuous questions: are you doing? | Present | A1
summary: Questions in the present continuous: Are you working? What is she doing? Short answers: Yes, I am. / No, he isn't.
[learn]
## Questions
Put **am / is / are** before the subject:
| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| You **are** working. | **Are** you working? |
| She **is** going out. | **Is** she going out? |
| They **are** coming. | **Are** they coming? |

= Are you listening to me?
= Is it raining?
= Are the children sleeping?

## With question words
question word + **am/is/are** + subject + **-ing**
= What **are** you do**ing**?
= Where **is** Sam go**ing**?
= Why **are** you laugh**ing**?
= Who **are** you talk**ing** to?
= What **is** she cook**ing**? It smells great!

## Short answers
| | |
|---|---|
| Is he working? | Yes, he **is**. / No, he **isn't**. |
| Are you coming? | Yes, I **am**. / No, I'**m not**. |
| Are they playing? | Yes, they **are**. / No, they **aren't**. |

> Word order: **Are you** ~~you are~~ watching TV? The verb *be* always goes before the subject in questions.
[examples]
What are you reading? — A detective story.
Is your phone ringing?
Are you enjoying the party? — Yes, I am.
Where are your friends going?
Why is the baby crying? — I think she's hungry.
Is Dev working today? — No, he isn't.
Who is she talking to on the phone?
What are you thinking about?

What you are doing? => What are you doing? :: Question word + are + you + -ing.
Is she work today? => Is she working today? :: Use the -ing form.
Are you coming? — Yes, I'm. => Are you coming? — Yes, I am. :: No short form in positive short answers.

[medium]

# 5 | Present simple: I do, I work, I like | Present | A1
summary: Use the present simple for habits, routines and facts. Add -s/-es with he, she and it: she works, he watches.
[learn]
## Form
| Subject | Verb |
|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | work, like, play |
| he / she / it | work**s**, like**s**, play**s** |

= I **drink** tea every morning.
= My father **reads** the newspaper after breakfast.
= Cats **sleep** a lot.

## Spelling with he / she / it
| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| most verbs + **-s** | work**s**, live**s**, play**s** |
| -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -o + **-es** | pass**es**, wash**es**, watch**es**, fix**es**, go**es**, do**es** |
| consonant + y → **-ies** | study → stud**ies**, try → tr**ies** |
| vowel + y → **-s** | play**s**, enjoy**s** |
| have → **has** | She **has** two brothers. |

## When do we use it?
- **Habits and routines**: I **get up** at 6. She **goes** to the gym on Fridays.
- **Facts and things that are always true**: Water **boils** at 100°C. The sun **rises** in the east.
- **Permanent situations**: They **live** in Pune. He **works** in a bank.
- **Likes and opinions**: I **like** mangoes. She **loves** music.

## Frequency words
**always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never** usually go before the main verb.
= I **always** have breakfast.
= Hari **often** plays cricket.
= We **never** eat meat.

> Use *every day / every week / on Mondays / twice a year* for how often: He calls his mother **every day**.
[examples]
I work in a hospital.
My sister speaks four languages. :: she → speaks
Water freezes at 0°C. :: a fact
He watches cricket every weekend. :: watch → watches
The shop opens at 9 o'clock.
She studies late every night. :: study → studies
We usually go to the beach in May.
My grandfather has a big garden. :: have → has
[mistakes]
She work in a bank. => She works in a bank. :: he/she/it → add -s.
He gos to school by bus. => He goes to school by bus. :: go → goes
My brother haves a car. => My brother has a car. :: have → has
[easy]
mc: My mother ___ English. | teach | *teaches | teachs :: she → teaches (-ch + es)
mc: We ___ near the station. | lives | *live | living :: we → live (no -s)
fill: He ___ (play) the guitar. = plays :: vowel + y → plays
fill: Mina ___ (go) to work by train. = goes :: go → goes
mc: The film ___ at 8 pm. | start | *starts | starting :: the film = it → starts
order: I drink coffee every morning. :: subject + verb + object + time
[medium]
fill: My son ___ (study) every evening. = studies :: consonant + y → ies
mc: The earth ___ round the sun. | go | *goes | is going :: fact → present simple
fill: She ___ (have) long hair. = has :: have → has
fix: He always watch TV after dinner. => He always watches TV after dinner. :: he → watches
fill: Our teacher ___ (fix) computers at the weekend. = fixes :: x → es
order: She often goes to the cinema. :: frequency word before the verb
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | Ravi enjoies music. | *Ravi enjoys music. | Ravi enjoy music. :: vowel + y → just -s
fix: My friends lives in London. => My friends live in London. :: plural → no -s
fill: A good teacher ___ (try) to help every student. = tries :: try → tries
mc: Nurses ___ care of sick people. | takes | *take | taking :: plural subject → take
fix: She never is late. => She is never late. :: frequency word AFTER be
fill: The museum ___ (close) at five on Saturdays. = closes :: museum = it → closes

# 6 | Present simple negative: I don't… | Present | A1
summary: Make negatives with don't (I/you/we/they) and doesn't (he/she/it) + the base verb.
[learn]
## Form
| Subject | Negative | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | **don't** (do not) | work / like / have |
| he / she / it | **doesn't** (does not) | work / like / have |

= I **don't** like coffee.
= She **doesn't** eat meat.
= They **don't** live here.

## Important: after *doesn't*, use the base verb
*Does* already has the -s, so the main verb has **no -s**.
| ✅ | ❌ |
|---|---|
| He doesn't **work**. | ~~He doesn't works.~~ |
| It doesn't **rain** much here. | ~~It doesn't rains.~~ |
| She doesn't **have** a car. | ~~She doesn't has a car.~~ |

## Compare positive and negative
= I **play** chess. → I **don't play** chess.
= He **speaks** French. → He **doesn't speak** French.
= My phone **works**. → My phone **doesn't work**.

> With *be* we do NOT use don't: I**'m not** tired (not ~~I don't be tired~~). She **isn't** here.

## Useful expressions
- I **don't know**.
- It **doesn't matter**.
- I **don't understand**. Can you say it again?
- He **doesn't mind** waiting.
[examples]
I don't drink tea in the evening.
My brother doesn't like spicy food.
We don't have a car. :: we → don't
This machine doesn't work. :: it → doesn't
They don't watch much TV.
Sara doesn't speak Hindi, but she understands it.
I don't know the answer.
It doesn't matter. Don't worry.
[mistakes]
She don't like fish. => She doesn't like fish. :: he/she/it → doesn't
He doesn't works on Sunday. => He doesn't work on Sunday. :: base verb after doesn't
I don't be hungry. => I'm not hungry. :: Use be + not, not don't.
[easy]
mc: I ___ like horror films. | doesn't | *don't | not :: I → don't
mc: She ___ live in the city. | don't | *doesn't | isn't :: she → doesn't
fill: We ___ (not / have) any pets. = don't have | do not have :: we → don't + base
fill: My dad ___ (not / drink) coffee. = doesn't drink | does not drink :: he → doesn't + base
mc: He doesn't ___ to music. | listens | *listen | listening :: base verb after doesn't
order: They don't work on Sundays. :: subject + don't + verb
[medium]
fix: Anil don't play cricket. => Anil doesn't play cricket. :: Anil = he → doesn't
fill: The bus ___ (not / stop) here. = doesn't stop | does not stop :: the bus = it
mc: I ___ understand this word. | am not | *don't | doesn't :: action verb → don't
fill: Kids ___ (not / like) going to the dentist. = don't like | do not like :: plural → don't
fix: It doesn't rains much in summer here. => It doesn't rain much in summer here. :: base verb
order: My sister doesn't eat breakfast. :: doesn't + base verb
[hard]
mc: Which is correct? | She doesn't has time. | *She doesn't have time. | She don't have time. :: doesn't + have
fix: I don't be late usually. => I'm not usually late. | I am not usually late. :: be → not
fill: That shop ___ (not / sell) newspapers any more. = doesn't sell | does not sell :: shop = it
mc: Our neighbours ___ very friendly. | don't | *aren't | doesn't :: friendly is an adjective → be
fix: He not speak Japanese. => He doesn't speak Japanese. :: he → doesn't speak
fill: My phone ___ (not / work) when it's cold. = doesn't work | does not work :: it → doesn't

# 7 | Present simple questions: Do you…? | Present | A1

[learn]
## Form
| | Subject | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| **Do** | I / you / we / they | work? like? live? |
| **Does** | he / she / it | work? like? live? |

= **Do** you like chocolate?
= **Does** your brother play football?
= **Do** they live near here?

## With question words
question word + **do/does** + subject + base verb
= Where **do** you work?
= What time **does** the train leave?
= How often **do** you go swimming?
= What **does** this word mean?
= Why **does** he always come late?

## Short answers
| | |
|---|---|
| Do you speak English? | Yes, I **do**. / No, I **don't**. |
| Does she drive? | Yes, she **does**. / No, she **doesn't**. |
| Do they know? | Yes, they **do**. / No, they **don't**. |

## Be careful with *be*
With *be* there is no do: **Are** you tired? **Is** he a doctor? (not ~~Do you be tired?~~)

Where you live? => Where do you live? :: Use do in questions.
Do he like tea? => Does he like tea? :: he/she/it → does

# 8 | Present continuous (I am doing) vs present simple (I do) | Present | A2
summary: Use the present continuous for now or temporary things, and the present simple for habits, facts and permanent things.
[learn]
## Two present tenses
| Present continuous (am/is/are + -ing) | Present simple (work / works) |
|---|---|
| happening **now** | happens **in general**, again and again |
| **temporary** | **permanent** |
| = Look! He**'s running**. | = He **runs** every morning. |
| = I**'m staying** with my aunt this week. | = I **live** with my parents. |

## Time words help you choose
| Continuous | Simple |
|---|---|
| now, right now, at the moment, today, this week, Look! Listen! | always, usually, often, every day, on Mondays, never |

= Arun **is watching** TV **right now**.
= Arun **watches** TV **every evening**.

## State verbs: usually NOT continuous
Some verbs describe states, not actions. We normally use them in the **present simple**, even about now:
**like, love, hate, want, need, know, understand, believe, remember, mean, prefer, belong, own, seem**
= I **want** a glass of water. (not ~~I'm wanting~~)
= Do you **understand** me now? (not ~~Are you understanding~~)
= This bag **belongs** to Maria.

> *Have* for possession is a state: I **have** a car. But *have* for actions can be continuous: I**'m having** lunch / a shower / a good time.

## Questions
= What **are** you **doing**? (now) — I'm cooking.
= What **do** you **do**? (your job) — I'm a nurse.
[examples]
Listen! Somebody is knocking at the door. :: now
She usually takes the bus, but today she's walking. :: habit vs today
I don't understand this question. :: state verb
We're having dinner. Can I call you later? :: have = eat → continuous OK
The river flows into the sea. :: fact
What do you do? — I'm a pilot. :: job question
Why are you wearing a coat? It's hot! :: now
I know your sister. :: state verb
[mistakes]
I am knowing the answer. => I know the answer. :: know is a state verb.
Look! It snows. => Look! It's snowing. :: Happening now → continuous.
She is going to work by car every day. => She goes to work by car every day. :: Habit → simple.

# 9 | I have… and I've got… | Present | A1
summary: Use have or have got for possession, family, illness and appearance. Negatives and questions are different for each.
[learn]
## Two ways to say the same thing
**have** and **have got** mean the same for things we own, family, appearance and illness.
| have | have got |
|---|---|
| I **have** a bike. | I**'ve got** a bike. |
| She **has** two sisters. | She**'s got** two sisters. |
| He **has** a cold. | He**'s got** a cold. |

> *Have got* is more common in spoken British English. In the past, use **had** (not had got).

## Negatives
| have | have got |
|---|---|
| I **don't have** a car. | I **haven't got** a car. |
| He **doesn't have** time. | He **hasn't got** time. |

## Questions and short answers
| have | have got |
|---|---|
| **Do** you **have** a pen? — Yes, I **do**. | **Have** you **got** a pen? — Yes, I **have**. |
| **Does** she **have** a dog? — No, she **doesn't**. | **Has** she **got** a dog? — No, she **hasn't**. |

## Don't mix them
= ✅ Do you have…? / Have you got…?
= ❌ ~~Have you a…?~~ ~~Do you have got…?~~

## *Have* for actions (NOT have got)
For actions we use only *have*: **have** breakfast/lunch/dinner, **have** a shower, **have** a party, **have** a good time, **have** a look.
= I **have** breakfast at 7. (not ~~I've got breakfast~~)
[examples]
I've got a headache. :: illness
My grandparents have a small farm.
She has got beautiful long hair. :: appearance
We don't have much time. :: negative with have
Has your flat got a balcony?
Do you have any brothers or sisters? — Yes, I do.
He hasn't got a passport yet.
They usually have lunch at one o'clock. :: action → only have
[mistakes]
Have you a pen? => Do you have a pen? | Have you got a pen? :: Use do or got.
She don't have a car. => She doesn't have a car. :: she → doesn't
I've got a shower every morning. => I have a shower every morning. :: Actions → have only.
